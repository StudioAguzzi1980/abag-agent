# 04 — Lead Qualification Checklist

## Objective

This checklist is completed **before** the discovery call. Its purpose is to confirm that the lead is worth pursuing and that the consultant is fully prepared. A lead that fails multiple criteria in this checklist should be deprioritized or placed in a nurture pipeline rather than fast-tracked to a sales conversation.

Use this as a practical gate: if you cannot check at least 8 of the 12 Core Qualification items, reconsider whether the timing is right for a full discovery call.

---

## Section A: Company Fundamentals

These are non-negotiable threshold criteria. If any of these fail, the lead is not currently qualified.

- [ ] **The company is legally incorporated in the United States** and operates as a legitimate, registered business entity.
- [ ] **The company has been operating for at least 12 months.** Pre-revenue or pre-product startups are very rarely ready for international expansion advisory services.
- [ ] **The company's primary industry or sector is one where ABAG has relevant expertise or demonstrated Italian/EU market entry experience.** *(See ABAG's key sectors: technology, manufacturing, fashion, food & beverage, professional services, financial services, healthcare, luxury goods.)*
- [ ] **There is no obvious regulatory or legal barrier** that would prevent the company from operating in Italy or the EU (e.g., pending U.S. legal proceedings, unresolved compliance issues, or a business model explicitly prohibited in the EU).

---

## Section B: Expansion Intent

- [ ] The company has expressed — directly or through observable signals — a genuine interest in entering Italy, another EU country, or Europe broadly.
- [ ] The expansion interest is connected to a **business objective** (revenue growth, client proximity, operational efficiency, regulatory compliance), not just a general curiosity or lifestyle aspiration.
- [ ] There is at least **one identifiable trigger** for the expansion (inbound demand from European clients, strategic mandate, competitive pressure, funding, regulatory requirement).
- [ ] The expansion timeline, while possibly undefined, is **within the next 12–24 months** — not a vague "someday."

---

## Section C: Financial Capacity

- [ ] Based on available information (revenue estimates, funding history, headcount), the company appears to have the **financial capacity to invest in an Italian or European setup** (minimum indicative budget: €20,000–€50,000+ for a basic structure; €100,000+ for a full commercial operation).
- [ ] There are **no visible signs of financial distress** (recent layoffs, bankruptcy filings, major executive departures, stalled growth).
- [ ] The company is **not in a survival or turnaround phase** where all capital is being directed at domestic stabilization.

---

## Section D: Decision-Making Access

- [ ] **You have identified — or have access to — a decision-maker** (founder, CEO, Managing Director, VP International, or equivalent) who has the authority to approve an international expansion project and commit budget.
- [ ] **The contact who initiated or will attend the meeting is not a gatekeeper only** (e.g., a junior sales or administrative contact with no authority). If this is the case, plan to identify and access the actual decision-maker during or after the call.
- [ ] **There is no known third party** (existing advisor, consultant, or law firm) already engaged on the same expansion initiative with a conflicting mandate.

---

## Section E: Consultant Preparation

These items confirm the ABAG consultant is ready for the conversation.

- [ ] The **Company Intelligence Summary** (from `01_Company_Analysis.md`) has been completed.
- [ ] The **Expansion Readiness assessment** (from `02_Expansion_Readiness.md`) has been reviewed and an initial hypothesis formed.
- [ ] **At least 5–7 questions** from the First Meeting Questions list (`06_First_Meeting_Questions.md`) have been selected and personalized for this specific company.
- [ ] The consultant is familiar with **at least one relevant ABAG case study, success story, or sector-specific example** they can reference during the conversation.
- [ ] A **clear opening statement** has been prepared that demonstrates ABAG's knowledge of this specific company and its situation (not a generic introduction).
- [ ] The consultant knows the **desired next step** from this call (e.g., proposal meeting, introductory advisory session, follow-up with additional stakeholders).

---

## Section F: Red Flags to Assess

These are not automatic disqualifiers, but each warrants careful consideration before proceeding.

- [ ] **Unrealistic timeline expectations:** Has the company expressed an expectation to be "operational in Italy within 30–60 days"? This may indicate a misunderstanding of the complexity involved.
- [ ] **Extreme price sensitivity early in the conversation:** A company that leads with "What's the cheapest way to do this?" before understanding what "this" involves may be difficult to serve profitably.
- [ ] **Multiple competing advisors:** Has the company mentioned that they are simultaneously talking to several other advisory firms? This may indicate a commoditized buying process where price will drive the decision.
- [ ] **Misaligned expectations about ABAG's role:** Does the contact appear to believe ABAG is a temporary setup service, not a strategic ongoing advisor? This expectation mismatch should be addressed proactively.
- [ ] **Personal motivation without business case:** Is the driving force behind the expansion purely the founder's personal desire to live in Italy? This can still be a viable engagement, but the business case must be established independently.

---

## Notes on Using This Checklist

This checklist is a tool for judgment, not a mechanical gate. A prospect who checks 13 of 14 items but where the one missing item is "financial capacity" requires a different response than one who checks 13 of 14 items but where the missing item is "the contact is a decision-maker."

Use professional judgment. When in doubt, a brief 15-minute exploratory call to assess financial capacity and decision-making authority is preferable to either pursuing an unqualified lead at full effort or dismissing a potentially strong opportunity prematurely.
