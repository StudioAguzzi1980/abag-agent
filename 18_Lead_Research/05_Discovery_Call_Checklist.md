# 05 — Discovery Call Checklist

## Objective

The discovery call is the most important moment in the ABAG business development process. It is your opportunity to listen deeply, ask the right questions, understand the prospect's real situation, and — when appropriate — present ABAG as the right partner.

This checklist is structured in three parts: **Before the Call**, **During the Call**, and **After the Call**. Each section has specific, actionable items.

---

## Part 1: Before the Call

Complete all of these items at least 24 hours before the scheduled call.

### Preparation

- [ ] Company Intelligence Summary (`01_Company_Analysis.md`) is complete and reviewed
- [ ] Expansion Readiness initial hypothesis (`02_Expansion_Readiness.md`) is formed
- [ ] Lead Qualification Checklist (`04_Lead_Qualification_Checklist.md`) has been completed — minimum 9/14 core items checked
- [ ] You have selected and personalized 8–10 questions from the First Meeting Questions list (`06_First_Meeting_Questions.md`)
- [ ] You know exactly who will be on the call (names, titles, roles) — research each attendee on LinkedIn
- [ ] You have a clear objective for this specific call:
  - [ ] Primary objective: _______________________________
  - [ ] Secondary objective: ______________________________
- [ ] You have identified the ideal next step you will propose at the end of the call
- [ ] You have prepared a 60-second opening statement that references something specific you know about this company (not a generic introduction)
- [ ] You have a relevant ABAG case study or example ready to reference if appropriate
- [ ] You have reviewed ABAG's service offerings and pricing range so you can speak to scope with confidence

### Logistics

- [ ] The call is scheduled on a calendar with the correct time zone confirmed (U.S. time zones vs. Italian/European time)
- [ ] You have the dial-in link or phone number and tested it in advance
- [ ] You have a quiet, professional environment for the call
- [ ] You have a notepad or CRM open and ready for live notes
- [ ] If a video call: background is professional, lighting is adequate, camera is on

## Part 2: During the Call

Use this as a live guide. Check items off mentally as the call progresses.

### Opening (First 5 Minutes)

- [ ] Start on time
- [ ] Introduce yourself and briefly describe ABAG in one or two sentences — do not lead with a lengthy pitch
- [ ] Confirm the attendees and their roles if not already known
- [ ] Briefly state the purpose and format of the call: *"I'd like to spend most of our time understanding your business and your goals — then I can share how ABAG may be able to help, and we can decide if it makes sense to go deeper."*
- [ ] Set a time boundary: confirm how long they have available
- [ ] Reference something specific you know about their company to demonstrate preparation: *"I noticed you recently expanded into the Southeast — congratulations. I'm curious how that experience is shaping your thinking about Europe."*

### Discovery Phase (Core of the Call — 20–35 Minutes)

This is the most critical section. **Listen more than you speak.** Your goal is to understand their situation deeply, not to sell.

- [ ] You are asking open-ended questions — not yes/no questions
- [ ] You are letting the prospect speak without interrupting
- [ ] You are actively taking notes on key facts, pain points, motivations, and language they use
- [ ] You have covered the following topic areas (not necessarily in this order):
  - [ ] **Current business situation** — What do they do, who are their customers, what is driving growth?
  - [ ] **Motivation for expansion** — Why Europe? Why now? Why Italy specifically (if applicable)?
  - [ ] **Timeline and urgency** — Is there a forcing function or deadline?
  - [ ] **Decision-making process** — Who else is involved? How do they typically evaluate and approve projects like this?
  - [ ] **Budget and financial parameters** — Have they allocated budget? What range are they working with?
  - [ ] **Previous experience** — Have they tried this before? What happened? Are they talking to other advisors?
  - [ ] **Success definition** — What does a successful outcome look like to them in 12–18 months?
- [ ] You have listened for — and noted — any objections, concerns, or hesitations expressed
- [ ] You have NOT made promises or commitments you cannot keep
- [ ] You have NOT presented a proposal or quoted fees unless explicitly asked and you are fully prepared to do so accurately

### Signals to Watch For During the Call

**Positive signals (buy indicators):**
- [ ] They ask about next steps unprompted
- [ ] They mention a specific deadline or trigger event
- [ ] They describe the problem in financial terms ("We're leaving money on the table in Italy")
- [ ] They mention that they've already spoken to others but haven't found the right partner
- [ ] They ask about ABAG's team, track record, or specific experience in their sector
- [ ] The decision-maker is personally engaged and asking follow-up questions

**Caution signals:**
- [ ] They seem distracted or multitasking during the call
- [ ] Vague or evasive answers about budget ("We haven't really thought about that yet")
- [ ] Repeated references to another advisor or firm already working on this
- [ ] The timeline is "maybe in 2–3 years"
- [ ] Only a junior contact on the call with no senior representation

### Closing the Call (Last 5–10 Minutes)

- [ ] Summarize the key points you heard: *"Based on what you've shared, here's what I'm understanding…"* — this demonstrates listening and invites correction
- [ ] Ask one direct summary question: *"Does that accurately capture where you are and what you're looking for?"*
- [ ] Share a brief, relevant perspective from ABAG's experience (not a pitch — an insight): *"What I find with companies at your stage is that the most common mistake is underestimating the timeline. Italy takes longer than people expect, but with the right structure in place, the results are worth it."*
- [ ] Propose a clear, specific next step:
  - *"I'd like to prepare a short scope of work for you — can we speak again in one week?"*
  - *"The logical next step would be a 30-minute session with our Italy market entry specialist. Would that be useful?"*
  - *"Let me put together a brief summary of what we discussed and what we'd recommend. I'll have that to you by Friday."*
- [ ] Confirm the next step on the call — date, time, format
- [ ] End on time

## Part 3: After the Call

Complete all of these items within 24 hours of the call.

### Documentation

- [ ] Full call notes entered into CRM or deal tracking system
- [ ] Company Intelligence Summary updated with new information learned
- [ ] Lead Qualification Checklist updated based on what was discovered
- [ ] Scoring Worksheet (`07_Scoring_System.md`) completed

### Follow-Up Actions

- [ ] A personalized follow-up email sent within 4 hours of the call (or by end of business at the latest), referencing:
  - [ ] Key points from the conversation
  - [ ] What you heard as their primary goal
  - [ ] The agreed-upon next step with a specific time/date
  - [ ] One concrete example of how ABAG has helped a similar company (if appropriate)
- [ ] Any materials promised during the call have been sent or are scheduled
- [ ] The next meeting or touchpoint is confirmed in both calendars

### Evaluation

- [ ] Opportunity recorded in pipeline with estimated value and probability
- [ ] Internal debrief scheduled if needed (for complex or high-value opportunities)
- [ ] Your own performance reviewed: What did you do well? What would you do differently?

## Call Quality Self-Assessment

After every discovery call, rate yourself honestly on these dimensions:

| Dimension | Rating (1–5) |
|---|---|
| Preparation: Did you know enough about the company going in? | |
| Listening: Did you talk less than 40% of the time? | |
| Question quality: Did you ask open-ended, insightful questions? | |
| Discovery depth: Do you fully understand their situation, motivation, and decision process? | |
| Next step: Did you close on a specific, agreed next step? | |
| Overall impression: Would you rate this call as "excellent," "good," or "needs work"? | |

A consistently high score across these dimensions is the mark of a world-class business development professional.
