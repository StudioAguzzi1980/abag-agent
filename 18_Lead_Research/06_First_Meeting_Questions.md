# 06 — The 20 Most Important Questions to Ask During a First Meeting

## How to Use This List

These are not a script. They are a bank of high-value questions from which you select and adapt based on what you already know about the company and how the conversation evolves.

The best first meetings feel like a genuine conversation, not an interrogation. Pick 8–10 questions from this list, customize them to the specific company, and let the answers guide the next question. Never ask a question you could have answered with five minutes of research — that signals poor preparation and wastes the prospect's time.

**The golden rule:** Ask one question at a time. Listen fully. Follow up on what you hear before moving to the next question.

---

## The 20 Questions

---

### 1. What's driving your interest in Italy or Europe — and why now?

**Why it matters:** This is the single most important question in any first meeting. The answer reveals the depth of intent, the strength of the business case, and whether the expansion is strategic or opportunistic. "Why now" is equally critical — it tells you whether there is urgency or just vague curiosity.

**Listen for:** Specific business drivers (existing clients, competitive pressure, regulatory triggers, funding mandate). Be cautious if the answer is purely personal or lifestyle-driven without a business rationale.

---

### 2. Tell me about your current business — who are your customers, and what problem do you solve for them?

**Why it matters:** You need to understand what they actually sell before you can assess Italy and Europe as markets for it. This also ensures you are not making assumptions based on their website.

**Listen for:** Clarity of value proposition, customer profile, and whether the offering translates to an Italian or European context.

---

### 3. Do you already have any customers, clients, or partners in Italy or Europe? If yes, tell me about them.

**Why it matters:** Existing European relationships are the single strongest indicator of expansion readiness. They validate market demand and often create urgency (a client asking for local presence, a partner expecting a formal structure).

**Listen for:** Named clients or partners, the stage of those relationships, and whether any of them are actively requesting a local ABAG presence.

---

### 4. What would success look like for you in the Italian market in 12 to 18 months?

**Why it matters:** This question reveals whether the prospect has a defined vision or a vague aspiration. A specific, measurable answer (e.g., "€500,000 in revenue from two enterprise clients") signals a company ready to invest seriously. An abstract answer (e.g., "just to have a presence") signals early-stage thinking.

**Listen for:** Specificity, realism, and whether their definition of success is something ABAG can help them achieve.

---

### 5. Have you explored other European countries as alternatives to Italy? What led you to focus on Italy?

**Why it matters:** This question serves two purposes. First, it reveals the depth of their market research. Second, if they haven't focused on Italy yet, it opens the door for ABAG to make the case for Italy as the right entry point.

**Listen for:** Whether they have a compelling reason to choose Italy (existing relationships, sector fit, personal connection) or whether they are still open to market guidance.

---

### 6. Has your company ever operated outside the United States before?

**Why it matters:** Prior international experience dramatically changes what the company needs from ABAG. A company that has already set up in the UK or Canada will have different questions than one that has never operated cross-border.

**Listen for:** What markets they've entered, what worked, what didn't, and what they learned. Past international experience — even if difficult — is a strong positive signal.

---

### 7. Who within your organization would own and drive this expansion initiative?

**Why it matters:** International expansion without a dedicated internal owner almost always fails. You need to understand whether they have the organizational bandwidth and commitment to execute, or whether they expect ABAG to run the entire initiative for them.

**Listen for:** A named individual, their seniority, and how much capacity they actually have. "I'll handle it on the side" is a red flag.

---

### 8. What does your decision-making process look like for a project of this kind? Who else needs to be involved?

**Why it matters:** This question is essential for understanding the sales process you are navigating. You may be speaking with a champion but not a decision-maker. The sooner you identify all stakeholders and their approval requirements, the better you can plan the path to a signed engagement.

**Listen for:** References to a board, co-founders, investors, or internal committees. Also note whether the person you are speaking with becomes defensive when asked this — which may signal they lack authority.

---

### 9. What is your expected timeline for getting the Italian entity established and operational?

**Why it matters:** Expectations about speed are one of the most common sources of friction in international expansion projects. Italian company formation and banking typically takes 4–12 weeks from document collection to operational readiness — not 5 days. Aligning on timeline early prevents disappointment.

**Listen for:** Specific deadlines (e.g., "We need to invoice an Italian client by March 1"). If a hard deadline exists, it creates urgency and often accelerates the decision process.

---

### 10. Have you thought about what type of legal structure you would want to use in Italy?

**Why it matters:** This reveals the sophistication of their thinking. A prospect who knows the difference between an SRL and a branch office has done their homework. One who has never heard of these structures needs education before they can make a decision.

**Listen for:** Whether they have preconceived ideas that may need to be adjusted (e.g., they've heard that a branch office is "simpler" without understanding the full liability implications).

---

### 11. What does your Italian — or European — go-to-market strategy look like? How do you plan to reach customers?

**Why it matters:** A legal structure without a go-to-market strategy is an expensive overhead. This question helps identify whether the company has a real commercial plan or just an administrative one — and whether ABAG's market entry strategy services are needed alongside company formation.

**Listen for:** Direct sales, partnerships, distributors, e-commerce, or a hybrid model. Vague or absent answers suggest this area needs work before or during the engagement.

---

### 12. Have you analyzed the competitive landscape in Italy for your product or service?

**Why it matters:** Understanding the Italian competitive environment is essential for any market entry strategy. This question also tests the depth of their preparation and whether they understand who they would be competing against.

**Listen for:** Named Italian or European competitors, their positioning, and any differentiated angle the prospect believes they bring to the market.

---

### 13. What do you see as the biggest risks or challenges in entering the Italian market?

**Why it matters:** This is one of the most revealing questions you can ask. It shows how self-aware the company is and what objections or concerns ABAG will need to address. It also demonstrates that you are a serious, realistic partner — not just a company that tells people what they want to hear.

**Listen for:** Realistic concerns (language, regulatory complexity, slow business pace, banking challenges) vs. unawareness. Both are useful data points.

---

### 14. Have you engaged with any other advisors, consultants, or law firms on this expansion?

**Why it matters:** You need to know if ABAG is competing for this engagement, and if so, with whom. You also need to understand whether there is an existing relationship that has created confusion or provided incorrect guidance that ABAG will need to address.

**Listen for:** Names of other firms, the nature of those relationships, and whether the prospect is still actively evaluating alternatives or has a preference.

---

### 15. What is the approximate budget you've set aside for the Italian setup and first year of operations?

**Why it matters:** Budget is one of the three criteria that determine whether to pursue an engagement (alongside authority and need). This is a critical question that many consultants avoid asking — and that avoidance costs them time and money.

**How to ask it professionally:** *"To make sure I can give you relevant guidance, it's helpful to understand the investment range you're working with — both for the setup itself and for the first year of operations. What range are you thinking?"*

**Listen for:** A specific range, a meaningful number (€20,000+ at minimum), or hesitation. If they genuinely don't know, offer a brief education on typical cost ranges to help them calibrate.

---

### 16. How does this Italian expansion fit into your broader company strategy over the next three to five years?

**Why it matters:** This question elevates the conversation from tactical to strategic. It also reveals whether Italy is a core part of the company's future or a peripheral experiment — which significantly affects the scope, depth, and longevity of the potential ABAG engagement.

**Listen for:** Italy as a gateway to broader European expansion, vs. Italy as a standalone objective. Companies with a 3–5 year European growth plan are typically ABAG's best long-term clients.

---

### 17. What is your current revenue and growth trajectory? (For private companies: What range are you comfortable sharing?)

**Why it matters:** While this may feel sensitive to ask, it is essential for assessing financial capacity and understanding whether the company is growing, stable, or declining. You can frame it as a question about context, not scrutiny.

**How to ask professionally:** *"To understand whether the timing is right, it's helpful to know where you are in your growth journey. Are you at a stage where international expansion would be additive, or is there a particular revenue milestone you want to hit first?"*

**Listen for:** Growth stage (startup, scale-up, established), revenue trends, and whether expansion is being funded from growth or is aspirational.

---

### 18. What does your current relationship with Italian or European culture look like — do any of your team members speak Italian or have European experience?

**Why it matters:** Internal cultural capital is a meaningful accelerator for market entry. A company with Italian-speaking team members, executives who have lived in Europe, or board members with European networks will have a fundamentally different experience entering Italy than one with zero existing ties.

**Listen for:** Languages spoken, prior experience, personal connections, and cultural openness. This also helps ABAG tailor its support — more cultural coaching for companies with no Italian background, less for those with deep existing ties.

---

### 19. What would "not working with ABAG" look like — what would you do instead?

**Why it matters:** This is a powerful question that reveals the prospect's alternatives and their assessment of ABAG's value. It may also reveal that they have already been given incorrect guidance by a non-specialist firm and are now looking for a more credible partner.

**How to frame it:** *"I always find it helpful to understand — if you don't move forward with a partner like ABAG, what does the alternative path look like for you?"*

**Listen for:** DIY attempts, engagement of a generic law firm, a friend's recommendation, or simply postponing the project indefinitely. Each alternative tells you something about how to frame ABAG's unique value.

---

### 20. Is there anything I haven't asked that you think is important for me to understand about your situation?

**Why it matters:** Every structured conversation has blind spots. This open invitation often surfaces the most important piece of information in the entire call — the thing the prospect has been waiting for the right moment to say.

**Listen for:** Personal motivations, time pressure, internal politics, specific concerns about the advisory process, or a decisive piece of context that reframes everything else you discussed.

---

## Bonus: Questions to Ask at the End of Every First Meeting

These three questions should become automatic in every closing:

1. **"On a scale of 1 to 10, how urgent is this for your business right now?"** — Direct, honest, and immediately revealing.
2. **"What would need to be true for you to feel confident moving forward with ABAG?"** — Surfaces remaining doubts or objections before they derail the process.
3. **"What's the best next step from your perspective?"** — Gives them ownership of the process and often results in a more committed next step than one you propose unilaterally.
