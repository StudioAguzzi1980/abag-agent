# 07 — ABAG Lead Scoring System (0–100)

## Overview

The ABAG Lead Scoring System provides a structured, consistent method for evaluating the likelihood that a U.S. company will become an ABAG client. It is designed to be completed after the discovery call, once you have gathered sufficient information about the prospect.

The system uses five weighted dimensions, each contributing a defined number of points to a total score out of 100. The final score informs the recommended action: active pursuit, nurture, or deprioritization.

---

## The Five Dimensions

| Dimension | Maximum Points | Weight |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Strategic Intent & Urgency | 25 | 25% |
| 2. Financial Capacity & Budget Alignment | 20 | 20% |
| 3. Decision-Making Authority & Process | 20 | 20% |
| 4. Product / Market Fit for Italy or Europe | 20 | 20% |
| 5. Cultural & Organizational Readiness | 15 | 15% |
| **Total** | **100** | **100%** |

---

## Dimension 1: Strategic Intent & Urgency (0–25 Points)

This dimension measures how clear, specific, and urgent the expansion intent is.

| Score | Description |
|---|---|
| **22–25** | The company has a specific, documented expansion plan for Italy or Europe. There is a hard deadline or forcing function (existing client requiring local presence, regulatory mandate, board approval). Leadership is personally committed. Expansion is a stated strategic priority. |
| **16–21** | Clear intent to expand, with a defined geographic target and a plausible business rationale. Timeline is approximate (6–18 months) but realistic. No hard deadline, but there is genuine momentum. |
| **9–15** | Exploratory stage. The company is actively researching options but has not committed to a plan. Interest is genuine but not yet urgent. |
| **4–8** | Early curiosity. The company is "thinking about" international expansion without a clear rationale, target, or timeline. |
| **0–3** | No real intent. Vague or speculative interest with no business case. May be driven by personal lifestyle aspirations rather than commercial logic. |

## Dimension 2: Financial Capacity & Budget Alignment (0–20 Points)

This dimension measures whether the company has the financial resources to fund the engagement and the subsequent expansion.

| Score | Description |
|---|---|
| **18–20** | Budget has been explicitly allocated and is commensurate with the scope (€50,000+ for a basic setup; €150,000+ for a full commercial launch). The company is in a strong financial position (funded, profitable, or growing). No financial distress indicators. |
| **13–17** | Budget is not yet formalized but the company is clearly financially capable (strong revenue, recent funding, growing headcount). When asked, they did not hesitate or deflect the question. |
| **7–12** | Budget exists but may be tightly constrained. The company is financially stable but conservative about investment. May need to start with a smaller initial scope. |
| **3–6** | Budget is vague or significantly below what is required. Financial situation is unclear or there are some indicators of stress. |
| **0–2** | No budget has been considered. The company appears unable or unwilling to invest at the required level. Financial stress is evident. |

## Dimension 3: Decision-Making Authority & Process (0–20 Points)

This dimension measures whether ABAG is speaking with the right person and whether the decision-making process is clear and manageable.

| Score | Description |
|---|---|
| **18–20** | You are speaking directly with the founder/CEO/Managing Director who has full authority to approve the engagement and budget. Decision process is simple and fast. No additional approval layers required. |
| **13–17** | Your primary contact is a senior decision-maker (VP International, COO, or equivalent) with strong influence. One additional approval step required (e.g., CFO sign-off or board notification). Process is clear and manageable. |
| **7–12** | Your contact is a champion but not the final decision-maker. You have been promised access to the decision-maker but it hasn't happened yet. The approval process involves multiple stakeholders or a formal committee. |
| **3–6** | Your contact is a mid-level manager or gatekeeper. You have not met or spoken with a decision-maker. The approval process is long, complex, or bureaucratic. |
| **0–2** | No clear path to a decision-maker. Your contact lacks influence. Decision process is opaque or appears to involve external parties (e.g., an investor who is resistant). |

## Dimension 4: Product / Market Fit for Italy or Europe (0–20 Points)

This dimension assesses whether the company's product or service has genuine commercial potential in Italy or the European market.

| Score | Description |
|---|---|
| **18–20** | Strong, validated market fit. The company already has paying customers or active commercial relationships in Italy or Europe. Their product/service is in a sector with clear Italian/EU demand and minimal regulatory barriers. |
| **13–17** | Good market fit hypothesis with supporting evidence. Inbound inquiries from European prospects, clear sector demand, existing partnerships, or industry precedents. Some localization needed but manageable. |
| **7–12** | Plausible market fit but unproven. The offering is relevant to the Italian market in theory, but no European commercial traction exists. Localization or regulatory compliance is needed. |
| **3–6** | Uncertain market fit. The product/service may require significant adaptation for Italy/EU. Regulatory barriers exist but have not been fully assessed. No Italian or European market evidence available. |
| **0–2** | Poor market fit. The product/service faces significant regulatory barriers, is unlikely to resonate with Italian/EU buyers, or the company cannot explain why Italy is the right market for what they offer. |

## Dimension 5: Cultural & Organizational Readiness (0–15 Points)

This dimension evaluates the company's internal readiness to navigate the Italian and European business environment.

| Score | Description |
|---|---|
| **13–15** | Strong readiness. Leadership has prior European or Italian business experience. Team members speak Italian or another European language. There is genuine cultural curiosity and respect for the complexity of the market. A dedicated internal resource is assigned to the expansion. |
| **9–12** | Moderate readiness. Some international experience exists, even if not specifically Italian. Leadership is open and realistic about the challenges. The company has managed multi-site or cross-border operations before. |
| **5–8** | Limited readiness. No prior international experience. No Italian-speaking team members. Leadership is enthusiastic but naive about Italian business culture and timelines. Adequate organizational bandwidth is questionable. |
| **2–4** | Low readiness. No international experience. Leadership expects Italian business to operate like the U.S. Unrealistic expectations about timelines, costs, or complexity. Expansion would likely be managed as an afterthought. |
| **0–1** | Very low readiness. Active resistance to adapting to Italian business norms. No organizational capacity for expansion. Expansion is being driven by personal interest without any business foundation. |

### Score Interpretation and Recommended Actions

| Score Range | Classification | Recommended Action |
|---|---|---|
| **80–100** | **Tier 1 — Hot Prospect** | Immediate priority. Move to proposal stage within 5 business days. Involve senior ABAG leadership if appropriate. |
| **65–79** | **Tier 2 — Strong Prospect** | High priority. Develop a tailored proposal within 10 business days. Address any specific gaps identified in the scoring. |
| **50–64** | **Tier 3 — Warm Prospect** | Moderate priority. Keep engagement active. Address key gaps (budget, authority, or readiness) before presenting a full proposal. Monthly follow-up cadence. |
| **35–49** | **Tier 4 — Early Stage** | Long-term nurture. Provide educational content, invite to ABAG events, and maintain light-touch contact. Re-evaluate in 3–6 months. |
| **Below 35** | **Not Currently Qualified** | Do not pursue active sales efforts. Offer a complimentary resource or article. Note the reason for disqualification and set a reminder to revisit in 6–12 months. |

## Scoring Modifiers

After calculating the base score, apply the following modifiers where relevant:

| Condition | Modifier |
|---|---|
| Company has been referred by an existing ABAG client | +5 |
| You have spoken with (and confirmed authority of) the CEO/founder directly | +3 |
| Company has a hard legal or contractual deadline for establishing EU presence | +5 |
| A competing advisory firm has already presented a proposal | −5 |
| Company has attempted Italian expansion previously and it failed | −5 (unless they can clearly explain what went wrong and have addressed it) |
| Company is in an ABAG priority sector (tech, F&B, luxury, manufacturing) | +3 |
| Company has Italian cultural connections (founder, key executive, or major investor) | +3 |

## Scoring Example

**Company: GreenTech Solutions Inc.** — U.S. cleantech SaaS company, $6M ARR, 45 employees, Series A funded.

| Dimension | Score | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| Strategic Intent & Urgency | 20 | Clear intent (2 Italian enterprise clients requesting local invoicing), 12-month timeline, not yet board-approved |
| Financial Capacity | 16 | Series A funded, €80K–€100K budget discussed, no financial stress |
| Decision-Making Authority | 14 | Speaking with VP Business Development — CEO meeting scheduled for next call |
| Product / Market Fit | 17 | Cleantech is a priority sector in Italy, 2 Italian clients already, some GDPR/regulatory work needed |
| Cultural & Organizational Readiness | 8 | No Italian speakers, but VP has worked in Germany previously, dedicated project owner assigned |
| **Base Total** | **75** | |
| Modifiers | +3 | Italian enterprise clients (treated as commercial traction signal) |
| **Adjusted Score** | **78** | |

**Classification:** Tier 2 — Strong Prospect. Develop tailored proposal within 10 business days. Priority: confirm CEO meeting and address regulatory compliance question before proposal.
