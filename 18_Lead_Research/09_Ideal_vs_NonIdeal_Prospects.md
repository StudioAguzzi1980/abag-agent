# 09 — Examples of Ideal and Non-Ideal Prospects

## Overview

Theory is useful, but pattern recognition is built through examples. This document presents detailed profiles of ideal and non-ideal prospects across a range of industries, sizes, and situations. Use these as calibration tools — the goal is not to memorize them, but to develop an instinct for what a good fit looks and feels like before you walk into a first meeting.

## Part 1: Ideal Prospect Profiles

---

### Ideal Prospect 1: The Funded SaaS Company with Italian Enterprise Clients

**Company:** CloudDocs Inc.
**Industry:** B2B SaaS — Document Management and Workflow Automation
**Size:** 60 employees | $7M ARR | Series A ($10M) closed 6 months ago
**Location:** Austin, TX

**Situation:**
CloudDocs has three Italian enterprise clients — two financial services firms and one manufacturing conglomerate — who have been using their platform via U.S.-based contracts for 18 months. Two of these clients have recently asked CloudDocs to establish an Italian legal entity so that contracts can be governed by Italian law and invoiced in euros. The CFO has been tasked by the board to evaluate the European expansion. The company has been approached by a generalist U.S. law firm but has not yet engaged anyone.

**Why This Is an Ideal Prospect:**

- **Pull demand, not push:** Italian clients are requesting the expansion — this is market pull, the strongest possible signal
- **Strategic alignment:** The board is behind the decision
- **Budget:** Series A funded, clear business case for the investment
- **Qualified authority:** CFO involved with board mandate — clear path to a decision
- **Sector fit:** SaaS with financial and manufacturing clients — two of Italy's strongest sectors
- **GDPR/tax urgency:** Italian clients and local operations will require immediate compliance work — ongoing retainer potential

**ABAG Score Estimate:** 85–92 / 100

**How to approach this prospect:** Lead with the Italian client situation. Don't pitch — ask how those client relationships are structured today and what the Italian clients are specifically requesting. Position ABAG as the expert who makes this transition smooth and fast.

### Ideal Prospect 2: The U.S. Food & Beverage Brand Entering Italy

**Company:** HarvestRoot Foods
**Industry:** Organic food products — specialty sauces, condiments, and preserves
**Size:** 25 employees | $4M revenue | Bootstrapped, profitable
**Location:** Portland, OR

**Situation:**
HarvestRoot has been selling at U.S. farmers markets and regional grocery chains for five years. Over the last 18 months, they have been approached by three Italian specialty food distributors who discovered their products at a trade show. The founder — Maria — has Italian grandparents from Tuscany and has visited Italy 12 times. She has hired an Italian-American sales consultant to evaluate the opportunity. The company is profitable and has $300K set aside for international growth.

**Why This Is an Ideal Prospect:**

- **Commercial traction:** Active inbound interest from Italian distributors (validated demand)
- **Personal motivation:** Founder's Italian heritage creates genuine commitment and cultural fluency
- **Financial capacity:** Profitable, cash available, realistic budget expectations
- **Industry fit:** Specialty food is a prestige category in Italy — premium U.S. brands with compelling stories perform well
- **Decision-making:** Founder-led business — Maria makes all major decisions
- **Cultural readiness:** Spoke Italian with grandparents, visited Tuscany multiple times

**ABAG Score Estimate:** 82–89 / 100

**How to approach this prospect:** Lead with personal connection and the Italian distributor relationships. Speak to Italy's food culture with genuine knowledge. Reference ABAG's experience with F&B market entry. Acknowledge that Italy is a market where trust and relationships take time — and that ABAG's network can accelerate this significantly.

### Ideal Prospect 3: The PE-Backed Business Services Company

**Company:** OpsBridge Solutions
**Industry:** Outsourced business process management (BPM) — HR, payroll, and compliance administration
**Size:** 120 employees | $18M revenue | Acquired by mid-market PE firm 14 months ago
**Location:** Chicago, IL

**Situation:**
OpsBridge was acquired by a Chicago-based PE firm that has a thesis around building a pan-European BPM platform. The PE firm has already established operations in Germany and the UK. Italy is identified as the next market in the rollout plan, with a 9-month target for operational readiness. The PE firm's operating partner has been tasked with managing the Italian expansion and has reached out to ABAG based on a referral from their German advisors.

**Why This Is an Ideal Prospect:**

- **PE mandate:** Expansion is investor-driven with a defined timeline — high urgency
- **Financial capacity:** PE-backed with explicit budget for the Italian initiative
- **Referral:** Introduction came through a trusted source — credibility already established
- **Timeline:** 9-month deadline creates urgency and a clear project scope
- **Decision-making:** Operating partner has authority and a clear mandate
- **Sector fit:** HR, payroll, and compliance services have immediate, obvious, and deep demand in Italy (Italian labor law is among the most complex in the EU)

**ABAG Score Estimate:** 88–95 / 100

**How to approach this prospect:** Respond quickly — PE-backed companies with defined timelines do not wait. Lead with ABAG's experience supporting multi-country rollouts and Italian labor law complexity. Prepare a structured scope of work within 72 hours of the first call.

### Ideal Prospect 4: The Healthcare Technology Startup with EU Regulatory Pressure

**Company:** ClinPath Analytics
**Industry:** Medical SaaS — clinical data analytics for hospital systems
**Size:** 30 employees | $3.5M ARR | Series Seed ($4M)
**Location:** Boston, MA

**Situation:**
ClinPath has been accepted into a prestigious EU health innovation accelerator and has been paired with a pilot partner hospital in Milan. The pilot has been running for 4 months. The hospital wants to convert to a paying customer but has legal and compliance requirements that necessitate ClinPath establishing an Italian legal entity and ensuring MDR (Medical Device Regulation) compliance. The CTO (who is the primary contact) is technically sophisticated but commercially inexperienced when it comes to international operations.

**Why This Is an Ideal Prospect:**

- **Hard requirement:** The paying customer relationship cannot be formalized without an Italian entity — this is a direct, non-discretionary trigger
- **EU regulatory compliance need:** MDR compliance is complex and requires specialized advisory support — creates additional ongoing retainer scope
- **Accelerator context:** Already embedded in the Italian health ecosystem
- **Small but focused:** 30 employees is small, but the business case is clear and immediate
- **Revenue-gated:** A paying €150K/year contract is contingent on ABAG's work being completed

**ABAG Score Estimate:** 80–86 / 100

**How to approach this prospect:** Lead with the hospital contract and the MDR regulatory requirement. Position ABAG as the partner that gets this done right the first time — the cost of mistakes in healthcare regulatory compliance is far higher than the cost of the advisory fee.

## Part 2: Non-Ideal Prospect Profiles

### Non-Ideal Prospect 1: The Lifestyle Entrepreneur

**Company:** SunsetVilla Rentals
**Industry:** Luxury vacation rental management
**Size:** 3 employees | $400K revenue | Bootstrapped
**Location:** Miami, FL

**Situation:**
The founder, James, has visited Tuscany twice and loves it. He wants to expand his vacation rental business to Italy. He has no Italian business contacts, no clients, no industry relationships, and no Italian language skills. His budget for "setting everything up" is approximately €5,000–€10,000. He is excited, enthusiastic, and expects to be operational within "a month or two."

**Why This Is a Non-Ideal Prospect:**

- **No commercial foundation:** No Italian clients, no partners, no market validation
- **Budget is insufficient:** €5,000–€10,000 does not cover legal setup, tax registration, accounting, and initial operations
- **Timeline is unrealistic:** Italian company formation and VAT registration alone typically takes 6–10 weeks
- **Small company with limited bandwidth:** A 3-person team cannot manage an international expansion while running an existing business
- **Personal motivation without business plan:** The driver is lifestyle, not commercial strategy

**ABAG Recommendation:** Do not pursue. Offer a complimentary 20-minute consultation to set realistic expectations. Recommend a preliminary market feasibility assessment if he wants to invest in building a real business case. Add to long-term nurture list and revisit in 18–24 months if the business grows.

**ABAG Score Estimate:** 18–25 / 100

### Non-Ideal Prospect 2: The Company in Turnaround

**Company:** RetailDrive Inc.
**Industry:** Retail technology — in-store customer analytics
**Size:** 80 employees (down from 150 two years ago) | $5M revenue (down from $9M three years ago)
**Location:** New York, NY

**Situation:**
RetailDrive is in the middle of a strategic pivot following the decline of their core retail market. The new CEO (hired 6 months ago) has proposed Italian expansion as a potential growth initiative. He has reached out to ABAG because he believes Italy's retail sector represents an opportunity for recovery. The company recently laid off 40% of its workforce and is in the process of renegotiating contracts with three major clients.

**Why This Is a Non-Ideal Prospect:**

- **Financial distress:** Revenue is declining, workforce has been cut, and existing client contracts are being renegotiated
- **Wrong use of international expansion:** Expansion cannot solve a domestic business model problem — it amplifies it
- **Leadership distraction:** A new CEO managing a turnaround has no bandwidth for international operations
- **No commercial traction:** No existing European clients or partnerships — expansion would be speculative
- **Motivated by desperation, not opportunity:** Companies that expand internationally to escape domestic problems consistently fail

**ABAG Recommendation:** Decline the active pursuit gracefully. Acknowledge the Italian opportunity is real, but be honest that ABAG's best results come when a company's domestic foundation is solid. Offer to reconnect in 12–18 months once the turnaround is complete.

**ABAG Score Estimate:** 22–30 / 100

### Non-Ideal Prospect 3: The Pure Tax Optimization Seeker

**Company:** WealthAdvisor Group LLC
**Industry:** U.S.-based financial planning and wealth management
**Size:** 10 employees | $2M revenue | Highly profitable
**Location:** Scottsdale, AZ

**Situation:**
The managing partner heard at a conference that establishing an Italian SRL could provide favorable tax treatment for profits from U.S. clients. He has no Italian clients, no Italian business activity, and no intention of conducting any commercial activity in Italy. He wants ABAG to set up a shell structure purely to reduce his U.S. tax liability.

**Why This Is a Non-Ideal Prospect:**

- **Illegal structure request:** A structure with no economic substance designed purely to minimize U.S. taxes on U.S.-source income is almost certainly illegal under U.S. tax law (particularly FATCA, GILTI, and the anti-abuse provisions of the U.S.–Italy tax treaty)
- **ABAG cannot assist:** This type of engagement would expose ABAG to professional liability and reputational risk
- **No Italian commercial purpose:** ABAG's core purpose is helping companies build real businesses in Italy — not creating tax shelter structures

**ABAG Recommendation:** Decline immediately and clearly. Explain that ABAG's services are designed to support companies with genuine commercial activity in Italy, and that structures without economic substance carry significant legal risk for the client under both U.S. and Italian law. Recommend they consult a qualified U.S. international tax attorney.

**ABAG Score Estimate:** Not applicable — disqualified on ethical and legal grounds.

### Non-Ideal Prospect 4: The "We Just Need the Paperwork" Prospect

**Company:** BrightSpark Consulting
**Industry:** U.S.-based management consulting
**Size:** 15 employees | $3M revenue | Stable
**Location:** Denver, CO

**Situation:**
BrightSpark has been asked by a U.S. multinational client to support a project in Italy. They need to invoice the client's Italian subsidiary in euros. They believe they just need a VAT number ("Partita IVA") and an Italian bank account. They have contacted ABAG specifically because they want someone to "just set up the paperwork" as quickly and cheaply as possible — they do not want strategic advice, ongoing compliance support, or any relationship beyond a one-time transaction.

**Why This Is a Marginal Prospect:**

- **Commoditized scope:** The company views ABAG as an administrative service, not a strategic advisor
- **Limited engagement potential:** A one-time VAT registration with no ongoing compliance or advisory work is not a profitable engagement for ABAG
- **Fee sensitivity:** Their stated priority is "quickly and cheaply" — this is a price-sensitive buyer
- **However:** There may be more here. The Italian subsidiary client relationship could grow. They may not realize what ongoing compliance obligations come with an Italian VAT number.

**ABAG Recommendation:** Do not dismiss outright. In the discovery call, explore whether the project in Italy is a one-time event or the beginning of a recurring relationship. Educate them on what an Italian VAT registration actually requires (annual filings, quarterly returns, potential permanent establishment risk). If they are willing to engage for at least an initial 12-month compliance package, they may be worth onboarding at a modest scope. If they insist on a one-time transaction only, refer them to a lower-cost administrative service.

**ABAG Score Estimate:** 35–45 / 100 — marginal, requires further qualification.

## Summary: Ideal vs. Non-Ideal at a Glance

| Characteristic | Ideal Prospect | Non-Ideal Prospect |
|---|---|---|
| Business driver for expansion | Commercial (clients, mandate, compliance) | Personal, aspirational, or desperation-driven |
| Financial capacity | Adequate budget committed or clearly available | Budget too small, undefined, or non-existent |
| Decision-making | Founder/CEO or senior executive with authority | Junior contact, gatekeeper, or absentee decision-maker |
| Market validation | Existing clients, partners, or inbound demand | Pure speculation, no European commercial traction |
| Timeline | Defined and realistic (6–18 months) | "Someday" or wildly unrealistic (30 days) |
| Expansion readiness | Sound domestic business, bandwidth available | Turnaround situation, team overloaded, or early-stage |
| Relationship potential | Multi-year advisory and compliance retainer | One-time transaction or disqualified entirely |
| Cultural readiness | International experience, realistic expectations | No international exposure, expects Italy = U.S. |
