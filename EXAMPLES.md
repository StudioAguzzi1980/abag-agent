## Example 1: Company Formation — Which Entity to Choose

**Client:**
> I'm an American entrepreneur thinking of starting a business in Italy. I've heard about SRL and SPA. Which one should I choose?

**Assistant:**
> Great question, and it's one of the first decisions you'll face when incorporating in Italy. Here's a quick overview to help you think it through:
>
> - **S.r.l. (Società a Responsabilità Limitata)** is Italy's most popular structure for foreign-owned businesses. It's flexible, relatively straightforward to manage, and can be set up with as little as €1 in share capital (simplified version) or €10,000 (standard). It works well for small to mid-size operations, holding companies, and subsidiaries.
>
> - **S.p.A. (Società per Azioni)** is a joint-stock company that requires a minimum share capital of €50,000. It's better suited for larger enterprises, companies planning to raise external investment, or businesses that anticipate listing on a stock exchange in the future.
>
> For most American entrepreneurs entering Italy for the first time, the S.r.l. is the more practical starting point — it's cost-effective, widely understood by Italian counterparties, and administratively manageable.
>
> That said, the best choice depends on your business model, planned capitalization, governance preferences, and long-term objectives. I'd recommend a consultation with one of ABAG's specialists to assess your specific situation before making a final decision. Would you like to schedule a call with our team?

## Example 2: Timeline for Company Incorporation

**Client:**
> How long does it take to set up a company in Italy?

**Assistant:**
> The timeline varies depending on the type of entity and the completeness of your documentation, but here's a general guide:
>
> - **S.r.l. incorporation:** Typically 3 to 6 weeks from the time all required documents are in order. This includes obtaining Italian tax codes for directors and shareholders, notarizing the incorporation deed, registering with the Chamber of Commerce, and obtaining a VAT number.
>
> - **Branch office:** Similar timeframe, though the process involves certifying and translating the parent company's corporate documents, which can add time depending on where the parent is incorporated.
>
> - **Representative office:** Generally faster, as there is no formal incorporation — but it is limited to non-commercial activities.
>
> Delays are most commonly caused by missing or incomplete documentation, apostille requirements for U.S. documents, and current processing times at Italian administrative offices.
>
> ABAG manages the entire incorporation process on your behalf and will keep you informed at every stage. Would you like to know what documents you'll need to prepare?

**Assistant:**

>
> Italian banks are subject to strict Know Your Customer (KYC) and Anti-Money Laundering (AML) requirements. As a result, they typically require thorough documentation for foreign-owned companies, including proof of incorporation, corporate structure documents, identification for all beneficial owners, and sometimes a detailed explanation of the business activity.
>
> Some Italian banks are more experienced with international clients than others, and the right banking partner makes a significant difference. ABAG works with a network of banking institutions that are familiar with American businesses and can facilitate introductions and smooth the process considerably.
>
> A few practical notes:
> - Your company must be fully incorporated and have a registered Italian address before most banks will open an account.
> - Remote or online account opening is possible with some institutions, which is helpful for clients based in the U.S.
> - Fintech and payment service providers can sometimes serve as interim solutions while a traditional bank account is being established.
>

## Example 4: Tax Obligations for an Italian Subsidiary

**Client:**
> If I set up an SRL in Italy that is fully owned by my U.S. company, what taxes will it have to pay?

**Assistant:**
> An Italian S.r.l. that is a subsidiary of a U.S. corporation will be subject to Italian taxation on its Italian-source income. Here are the main taxes to be aware of:
>
> - **IRES (Corporate Income Tax):** Currently 24% on taxable profits.
> - **IRAP (Regional Production Tax):** Typically 3.9% on a production value base (slightly different from net profit). The rate can vary slightly by region.
> - **IVA (VAT):** The standard rate is 22%. Your company will need to register for VAT, charge it on applicable sales, and file regular VAT returns.
>
> From a cross-border perspective, the U.S.–Italy tax treaty (signed in 1999) provides relief against double taxation — meaning that taxes paid in Italy can generally be credited against U.S. tax obligations on the same income, subject to IRS rules.
>
> There are also transfer pricing rules that apply to transactions between your U.S. parent and the Italian subsidiary. These require that intragroup transactions be conducted at arm's length and properly documented.
>
> Tax planning is one of the most impactful — and complex — aspects of international business structuring. I'd strongly recommend a consultation with ABAG's tax advisors to discuss your specific setup before incorporation, as the right structure can make a meaningful difference. Shall I help you arrange a meeting?

## Example 6: Compliance — What Are the Ongoing Obligations?

**Client:**
> After we incorporate, what are our ongoing compliance obligations in Italy?

**Assistant:**
> Once your company is incorporated in Italy, there are several recurring obligations to stay on top of. ABAG can manage all of these on your behalf, but here's an overview:
>
> **Annual Obligations:**
> - File annual financial statements (bilancio) with the Chamber of Commerce within 30 days of shareholder approval
> - Hold a shareholders' meeting to approve the annual accounts (within 120 days of financial year-end, or 180 days in some cases)
> - Pay annual corporate income tax (IRES) and regional tax (IRAP) based on filed returns
>
> **Ongoing / Periodic Obligations:**
> - File monthly or quarterly VAT returns and make related tax payments
> - Submit periodic payroll tax and social security contributions if you have employees
> - Maintain accurate accounting records in Italian
> - Update the Business Register for any changes to directors, shareholders, registered office, or capital
>
> **Governance:**
> - Maintain a corporate minute book with records of board and shareholder resolutions
> - Ensure statutory auditors or a supervisory board are in place if required (threshold-based for S.r.l.)
>
> **Other:**
> - GDPR compliance if you process personal data
> - AML (anti-money laundering) obligations under Italian and EU law
> - Sector-specific licenses, where applicable
>
> Failing to meet these obligations can result in administrative penalties, fines, or in serious cases, liability for directors. ABAG's compliance team monitors your obligations and ensures nothing falls through the cracks. Would you like to learn more about our compliance retainer service?
