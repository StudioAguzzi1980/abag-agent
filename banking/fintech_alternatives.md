# Italian Banking — Fintech and Alternative Payment Solutions

## Overview

Traditional Italian bank accounts are essential for Italian operations, but fintech and alternative payment solutions are increasingly viable supplements — and in some cases, suitable alternatives for specific transaction types. This guide covers the main options available to U.S.-owned Italian companies.

---

## Why Consider Fintech Solutions?

| Benefit | Description |
|---|---|
| **Speed of onboarding** | Fintech accounts can often be opened in days vs. weeks or months for traditional banks |
| **Digital-first experience** | Designed for online management; English interfaces available |
| **Multi-currency** | Easily hold USD, EUR, and other currencies in one account |
| **Competitive FX rates** | Significantly lower conversion fees than traditional banks |
| **Global SEPA + SWIFT** | Most support both EU domestic and international transfers |
| **Lower fees** | Generally lower monthly fees and transaction costs |

## Key Platforms and Their Suitability

### Wise Business (formerly TransferWise)
- **Type:** FCA/EMI regulated; not a full bank
- **Italian IBAN:** Yes
- **SEPA payments:** Yes
- **International transfers:** Yes, competitive rates
- **VAT invoicing:** Via integrations
- **Best for:** Companies with frequent USD/EUR conversions; international payments
- **Limitation:** Not accepted by all Italian counterparties; no overdraft or credit products

### Qonto
- **Type:** French EMI regulated; Italian market focus
- **Italian IBAN:** Yes (Italian-format IT IBAN)
- **SEPA payments:** Yes
- **Italian-specific features:** Integrated expense management; SDI electronic invoicing integration
- **Best for:** Italian SMEs and startups wanting a digital-first experience with Italian compliance features
- **Limitation:** Limited international/non-EUR functionality compared to Wise

### Revolut Business
- **Type:** EU-licensed bank (Lithuania); expanding European presence
- **Italian IBAN:** Available (IBAN format may vary)
- **SEPA payments:** Yes
- **Multi-currency:** Excellent
- **Best for:** Companies managing multiple currencies; international teams
- **Limitation:** Customer support quality varies; may not be accepted for all Italian B2B transactions

### N26 Business
- **Type:** German licensed bank
- **Italian IBAN:** Yes (German IBAN format — DE; may not be accepted by all Italian platforms)
- **SEPA payments:** Yes
- **Best for:** Simple EUR account management
- **Limitation:** Italian-format IBAN not available; some Italian partners reject non-IT IBANs

### Sella Bank
- **Type:** Full Italian bank (traditional, but digital-friendly)
- **Italian IBAN:** Yes (IT format)
- **English interface:** Partial
- **Best for:** Companies wanting a regulated Italian bank with more digital features
- **Limitation:** Similar onboarding requirements to traditional banks

## Italian-Format IBAN

Many Italian businesses, suppliers, and government platforms **require an IT (Italian) IBAN**. Non-IT IBANs (e.g., DE, FR, LT) are technically valid for SEPA but may be rejected by:
- Some Italian government portals (e.g., F24 tax payment platform — must use Italian bank)
- Some Italian corporate clients with rigid supplier onboarding systems
- Some Italian payroll processing systems

**Recommendation:** Always maintain an Italian-format IBAN from an Italian bank for official purposes (tax payments, statutory filings). Fintech accounts can supplement but not fully replace.

---

## Tax Payment Requirements

**Italian tax payments (F24)** — including IRES, IRAP, IVA, INPS contributions — must be made through an **Italian bank or postal account** (Banca d'Italia). These cannot be paid from a foreign account or fintech account that lacks Italian bank connectivity.

This is the single most important reason to maintain a traditional Italian corporate bank account alongside any fintech solution.

---

## Recommended Approach

For most U.S.-owned Italian companies, ABAG recommends:

1. **Primary account:** Traditional Italian bank (IT IBAN) — used for Italian tax payments, major Italian client receipts, and employee payroll
2. **Secondary account:** Wise Business or Revolut Business — used for USD/EUR conversions, international payments to U.S. parent, and operational day-to-day transactions where applicable

This hybrid approach maximizes efficiency while ensuring full Italian compliance.

## Payment Service Providers (PSPs) for E-commerce

For Italian companies operating e-commerce platforms:

| PSP | Use Case |
|---|---|
| **Stripe** | Card processing; excellent API; widely used in Italy |
| **PayPal Business** | Widely recognized; B2C and B2B use |
| **Nexi** | Major Italian payment processor; covers Italian card brands (Bancomat Pay) |
| **Satispay** | Italian P2P and retail payments; popular with local consumers |
| **Klarna / Scalapay** | Buy-now-pay-later; growing in Italian e-commerce |
