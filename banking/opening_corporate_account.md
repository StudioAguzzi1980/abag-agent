# Opening a Corporate Bank Account in Italy

## Overview

Opening a corporate bank account is an essential step in the Italian market entry process. Without a bank account, the company cannot receive payments from Italian customers, pay Italian employees or suppliers, or pay Italian taxes.

Despite being essential, the account opening process is consistently the most challenging and time-consuming element of establishing an Italian operation — particularly for foreign-owned companies. ABAG's banking support service is specifically designed to navigate this process efficiently.

---

## When to Begin the Bank Account Process

**Start the banking process in parallel with company formation.** Do not wait until incorporation is complete before beginning document preparation.

Key timing considerations:
- Document preparation for banking can begin as soon as the company structure is decided
- Bank introductions can be scheduled once documents are ready (even before full registration)
- Some banks require the company to be fully registered before opening; others allow the process to begin earlier
- Target: account open within 2–4 weeks of completing company registration

---

## Who Can Open an Italian Corporate Bank Account?

Any legally registered Italian company (S.r.l., S.p.A., branch office) can open a corporate bank account. However:

- **Pre-requirement:** Company must be fully registered in the Italian Business Register
- **Pre-requirement:** Italian tax code (codice fiscale) and VAT number (Partita IVA) are required
- **Pre-requirement:** A registered Italian address is required (ABAG can provide this)

## Documentation Required

### Company Documents

- [ ] Visura Camerale (Business Register extract, dated within 3 months)
- [ ] Atto Costitutivo and Statuto (incorporation deed and bylaws)
- [ ] Latest approved financial statements (if the company has been operating)
- [ ] Company description letter (business activity, clients, purpose) in Italian and English
- [ ] Board resolution authorizing the bank account opening and designating authorized signatories
- [ ] List of beneficial owners with respective shareholding percentages

### Individual Documents (Directors, Authorized Signatories, Beneficial Owners)

- [ ] Valid passport (color copy)
- [ ] Proof of address (utility bill or bank statement, less than 3 months old)
- [ ] Codice fiscale for each individual
- [ ] Source of funds declaration (some banks require formal statutory declaration)

### For U.S. Corporate Shareholders (Additional)

- [ ] U.S. parent company Certificate of Incorporation (apostilled + Italian translation)
- [ ] U.S. parent company Articles/Bylaws (apostilled + Italian translation)
- [ ] U.S. parent Certificate of Good Standing (apostilled + Italian translation)
- [ ] Organizational chart showing the complete ownership structure

## Bank Selection Factors

Not all Italian banks are equally suited to foreign-owned companies. ABAG evaluates:

| Factor | Considerations |
|---|---|
| **Experience with foreign clients** | Banks with international divisions are more accustomed to non-EU documentation |
| **English-language service** | Reduces communication friction |
| **International wire transfer capability** | Essential for USD/EUR transactions with U.S. parent |
| **SEPA and cross-border payment fees** | Compare transaction costs across banks |
| **Online banking quality** | English-language online banking is increasingly available |
| **Account opening speed** | Some banks are known for faster processes |
| **Physical proximity** | Branch accessibility for initial meetings and signatory requirements |

ABAG maintains relationships with specific banking institutions that have demonstrated consistent capability in onboarding foreign-owned Italian companies.

## The Account Opening Meeting

Most Italian banks require at least one meeting with a bank representative. This meeting typically covers:
- Presentation of all documentation
- Interview regarding the business activity, expected transactions, and funding sources
- Due diligence Q&A on the ownership structure and beneficial owners
- Signing of account opening forms and mandates

**ABAG facilitates this meeting** by:
- Preparing the complete documentation package
- Briefing the bank in advance
- Attending (or preparing the client to attend) the meeting
- Following up on any outstanding documentation requests

## Remote Account Opening

Some banks allow partial or fully remote account opening for foreign clients:
- Video call KYC (increasingly common post-COVID)
- Acceptance of electronically certified documents
- Digital signatures on account opening documentation

ABAG identifies which banking partners offer remote options and recommends them where appropriate for U.S.-based clients who cannot travel to Italy.
