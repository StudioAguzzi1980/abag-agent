# Case Study: U.S. Manufacturing Company — Italian Subsidiary

## Client Profile

| | |
|---|---|
| **Industry** | Precision manufacturing / Industrial components |
| **Headquarters** | Columbus, OH |
| **Revenue** | ~$85M annually (U.S.) |
| **Italy objective** | Establish an Italian subsidiary to serve European manufacturing clients and manage a small local production/assembly operation |
| **Timeline** | Operational within 120 days; first Italian employee within 6 months |

---

## Challenge

The client had secured a multi-year supply contract with an Italian tier-1 automotive supplier that required the company to have an Italian entity for contractual, invoicing, and warranty purposes. The company's CFO needed the Italian entity to be fully compliant, GAAP-reportable to HQ, and operational before the first Italian delivery date.

Key challenges:
1. U.S. parent had IRES/transfer pricing concerns from prior experience with a Mexican subsidiary
2. CFO required Italian financial statements in a format compatible with U.S. GAAP consolidation
3. Company needed to hire 3 Italian engineers (skilled workers) immediately after formation
4. Italian client required an Italian bank account for EUR wire transfers
5. Company had previously worked with a Big 4 firm that quoted very high fees and slow timelines

---

## ABAG Solution

### Pre-Formation Tax Planning (Weeks 1–2)

ABAG's tax team conducted a pre-formation tax analysis covering:
- Optimal legal structure (S.r.l. recommended; sufficient for manufacturing subsidiary)
- Share capital recommendation (€50,000 to demonstrate financial solidity to Italian bank and clients)
- Transfer pricing approach for intercompany goods sales (Cost Plus method documented in advance)
- U.S.–Italy withholding tax analysis on expected dividend distributions
- IRAP implications given the planned employee headcount

**Key finding:** With the Cost Plus intercompany pricing approach and proper documentation, the effective combined Italian tax rate was modeled at 26–28% — acceptable given the treaty-protected dividend distribution.

### Company Formation (Weeks 2–7)

- Codice fiscale obtained for 2 U.S. directors in Week 2
- Share capital of €50,000 deposited via ABAG's notarial account
- Incorporation deed executed in Week 6; Partita IVA in Week 7
- Share capital fully paid up at incorporation (waived 25% minimum requirement)
- Registered office provided by ABAG pending the client's lease negotiation

### Banking (Weeks 5–11)

- Documentation package prepared from Week 5 (in parallel with formation)
- Introduction to an Italian bank with manufacturing sector experience in Northern Italy
- Bank meeting in Week 8 (two ABAG representatives and client CFO by video)
- Account active in Week 11

### Accounting and Reporting Setup (Weeks 7–14)

- Italian GAAP accounting system configured in Xero (Italy edition)
- Monthly management pack template created: Italian trial balance + English management P&L + intercompany reconciliation
- First VAT return prepared and filed in Month 3
- Transfer pricing file prepared before first intercompany transaction
- Italian Statutory Accounts (bilancio) prepared and filed for the first year

### HR and Employment (Weeks 8–16)

- CCNL Metalmeccanico identified as the applicable collective agreement
- Employment contracts drafted for 3 engineers
- Centro per l'Impiego pre-hire notifications filed
- INPS and INAIL registrations completed
- Safety assessment (DVR) coordinated with an Italian H&S specialist

---

## Key Lessons

- **Transfer pricing must be documented before the first intercompany transaction**, not after — the client's prior Mexican subsidiary experience had resulted in a costly retrospective adjustment
- **IRAP is a significant cost for labor-intensive businesses** — the manufacturing model with 4 employees added ~1.5% to effective tax rate vs. a pure holding or service model
- **CCNL Metalmeccanico** is one of the most generous CCNLs in Italy — employer must budget accordingly for total employment costs
- **Bank relationship selection matters** — the bank chosen had specific sector knowledge and processed the account faster than average
