# Case Study: U.S. Professional Services Firm — Italian Office

## Client Profile

| | |
|---|---|
| **Industry** | Management consulting |
| **Headquarters** | Chicago, IL |
| **Size** | 120 consultants (U.S.); privately held |
| **Italy objective** | Open a Milan office to serve Italian and EU clients; secondment of 2 U.S. partners; hiring 4 Italian consultants |
| **Timeline** | Operational in Milan within 4 months |

---

## Challenge

The firm had grown a significant number of Italian and European client relationships from Chicago and was losing opportunities to local competitors who could offer in-person presence. The Managing Partners decided to open a Milan office as a service hub for European clients.

Key challenges:
1. Two U.S. partners would be relocated to Italy — creating complex personal tax exposure (U.S. citizens, Italian residents)
2. Four Italian consultants needed to be hired — CCNL selection was unclear (consulting/services)
3. The firm handled significant amounts of client personal and business data — GDPR obligations were unknown
4. Partners were concerned about the implications of being Italian tax residents while remaining U.S. taxpayers
5. Previous advisors (a U.S. law firm) had quoted an 8-month setup timeline — the firm needed to move faster

---

## ABAG Solution

### Personal Tax Planning for Relocated Partners (Weeks 1–3)

ABAG's tax team addressed the U.S.–Italy personal tax intersection for both relocating partners:
- Confirmed Italian tax residency would trigger worldwide income reporting to Italy
- Analyzed availability of the **Regime degli Impatriati** (50% Italian income exemption) for both partners
- Both partners qualified under the 3-year non-residency rule; significant Italian tax savings confirmed
- Coordinated briefing with the firm's U.S. CPA on FBAR, Form 2555, and Foreign Tax Credit planning
- Recommended the New Resident Flat Tax was not applicable (partners had Italian-source employment income as the primary income — Impatriati regime better suited)

### Company Formation (Weeks 2–7)

- Italian S.r.l. established with the U.S. LLP as 100% shareholder
- Registered office in central Milan (provided by ABAG initially; client took their own premises in Month 4)
- Director: one of the relocating U.S. partners

### CCNL and HR Framework (Weeks 4–10)

- CCNL Studi Professionali identified as the applicable agreement for consulting staff
- Employment contracts drafted for 4 Italian consultants
- Italian-language employee handbook prepared
- INPS/INAIL registrations completed
- Health and safety assessment arranged

### GDPR Compliance (Weeks 6–12)

Given the sensitive nature of management consulting (client financial and strategic data), ABAG conducted a full GDPR compliance review:
- Data mapping of all processing activities (client data, employee data, marketing data)
- RoPA (Records of Processing Activities) prepared
- Privacy policies for clients, employees, and website visitors drafted in Italian and English
- Data Processing Agreements issued to key software vendors (Microsoft, CRM, video conferencing)
- Employee GDPR training delivered
- Data Transfer Impact Assessment (DTIA) for data flows to U.S. parent prepared

### Banking (Weeks 5–11)

- Standard Italian corporate account opened with bank experienced in professional services firms
- Account active in Week 11

| GDPR enforcement actions | None |

---

## Key Lessons

- **Impatriati regime is underutilized** — many U.S. professionals relocating to Italy don't know they qualify; the tax saving is substantial
- **GDPR for professional services is non-trivial** — client data, cross-border data flows, and cloud services all require careful documentation
- **CCNL Studi Professionali** provides relatively flexible employment terms compared to other CCNLs — well-suited for consulting firms
- **Timeline compression is possible** with focused coordination — 14 weeks vs. the 8-month estimate from a U.S. law firm reflected ABAG's Italy-specific relationships and parallel workstreams
