# Case Study: Tech Startup Expansion to Milan

## Client Profile

| | |
|---|---|
| **Industry** | SaaS / B2B technology |
| **Headquarters** | New York, NY |
| **Size** | 45 employees (U.S.); Series B funded |
| **Italy objective** | Establish Italian subsidiary to serve European enterprise clients; hire 3 Italian sales/support employees |
| **Timeline** | Operational in Italy within 90 days of engagement |

---

## Challenge

The client was a fast-growing SaaS company that had secured several large European enterprise contracts — two of which were with Italian corporates. Their European clients were requesting an Italian entity for contracting and invoicing purposes, and the company was keen to build a local sales team to accelerate growth across Southern Europe.

The client had no prior Italian experience and no Italian-speaking staff at the time. Their General Counsel was a U.S.-trained attorney unfamiliar with Italian corporate law. Initial efforts to engage a Milan law firm had resulted in delays and proposals that didn't address the company's U.S.-specific reporting requirements.

**Key challenges identified:**
1. Urgent timeline driven by client contract requirements
2. Need for Italian entity that could invoice immediately upon establishment
3. U.S. parent company accounting team required Italian financials aligned with U.S. GAAP reporting
4. No knowledge of Italian employer obligations before hiring first Italian employee
5. Difficulty getting responses from traditional Italian banks

---

## ABAG Solution

ABAG was engaged as the single point of coordination for the entire Italian market entry.

### Phase 1 — Company Formation (Weeks 1–6)

- Discovery call and structure selection: ABAG recommended a standard S.r.l. with the U.S. parent as 100% shareholder
- ABAG applied for codice fiscale for the two U.S. directors within 3 days of engagement
- Coordinated apostille and translation of the U.S. parent's corporate documents
- Arranged a Milan notary with availability within the required timeline
- Incorporation deed executed in Week 5; Business Register registration and Partita IVA within Week 6

### Phase 2 — Banking Support (Weeks 4–10)

- Preparation of the full KYC documentation package began in Week 4 (in parallel with formation)
- ABAG introduced the client to a Milan-based bank with dedicated international business services
- Bank meeting facilitated in Week 7 (via video call due to client's inability to travel)
- Account active with IBAN issued in Week 10

### Phase 3 — Tax and Accounting Setup (Weeks 6–12)

- ABAG configured accounting software to support monthly management accounts in English for the U.S. parent
- Set up electronic invoicing (SDI) system compatible with the client's enterprise billing software
- Prepared first Italian VAT registration and filing
- Transfer pricing memo drafted before first intercompany transaction (management fee)

### Phase 4 — First Employee Onboarding (Weeks 8–14)

- ABAG identified the correct CCNL (Terziario/Commercio) for the sales role
- Drafted employment contract and provided template offer letter
- Completed all pre-hire Centro per l'Impiego notifications
- Handled INPS and INAIL registrations
- Provided initial payroll setup and first payslip generation

---

## Key Lessons

- **Parallel workstreams matter:** Overlapping formation and banking processes saved 4–6 weeks vs. sequential approach
- **U.S. parent reporting setup must begin at incorporation:** Retrofitting the accounting structure is significantly more expensive
- **Employment onboarding lead time is underestimated:** Two weeks from contract offer to active employee is ambitious in Italy; budget 4–6 weeks
- **Having an Italian registered address from day one** eliminated multiple administrative bottlenecks
