# Italian Company Formation — Timeline Guide

## Overview

The timeline for incorporating an Italian company depends on several factors, including the type of entity, the completeness and speed of document preparation, and the current workload of Italian administrative authorities.

This guide provides realistic timeline expectations for each stage of the incorporation process.

---

## Standard S.r.l. Formation Timeline

| Phase | Description | Estimated Duration |
|---|---|---|
| **Phase 1: Discovery and planning** | Discovery call, structure selection, preparation of document checklist | 3–5 business days |
| **Phase 2: Engagement and document collection** | Engagement letter signed; client gathers required documents | 1–3 weeks (client-dependent) |
| **Phase 3: Codice fiscale applications** | ABAG applies for Italian tax codes for directors and shareholders | 5–10 business days |
| **Phase 4: Document review and preparation** | ABAG reviews client documents; prepares draft atto costitutivo and statuto | 5–7 business days |
| **Phase 5: Notarial deed** | Notary reviews documents and executes the deed | 3–7 business days (subject to notary availability) |
| **Phase 6: Chamber of Commerce registration** | Notary files with Chamber of Commerce; company enters Business Register | 7–15 business days |
| **Phase 7: VAT registration** | Agenzia delle Entrate issues VAT number (Partita IVA) | 3–10 business days |
| **Phase 8: Bank account opening** | Document preparation, bank introduction, account activation | 4–8 weeks (overlapping with above) |
| **Total (incorporation only)** | From signed engagement to VAT number | **4–8 weeks** |
| **Total (incorporation + bank account)** | From signed engagement to operational bank account | **8–14 weeks** |

## Branch Office Timeline

| Phase | Estimated Duration |
|---|---|
| Discovery, planning, and engagement | 1 week |
| U.S. parent document preparation and apostille | 2–4 weeks (client-dependent; apostille can be slow) |
| Notarial deed and Chamber of Commerce registration | 2–3 weeks |
| VAT registration | 1–2 weeks |
| **Total** | **5–9 weeks** |

*Note: Apostilling U.S. corporate documents is frequently the main delay for branch office registrations. Start this process as early as possible.*

## What Causes Delays?

The most common causes of incorporation delays (in order of frequency):

1. **Incomplete or missing client documents** — The client is unable to provide required documents quickly (especially U.S. corporate documents needing apostilles)
2. **Apostille processing time** — Some U.S. states have significant backlogs for apostille processing; some states are faster (California vs. New York, for example)
3. **Codice fiscale for non-EU individuals** — Occasionally delayed by Agenzia delle Entrate processing queues
4. **Notary availability** — Italian notaries can be busy; availability in the client's chosen city may vary
5. **Chamber of Commerce processing** — Generally predictable but occasionally slower during peak periods
6. **Bank account opening** — Consistently the most time-variable step; highly dependent on bank and documentation quality

---

## Tips to Minimize Timeline

1. **Start the apostille process immediately** — This is usually the longest-lead-time item for U.S. clients
2. **Prepare documents in parallel** — Don't wait for one step to complete before starting the next
3. **Use expedited apostille services** — Several commercial services offer faster apostille processing in most U.S. states
4. **Respond quickly to ABAG document requests** — Each delay in responding pushes the timeline back by at least several business days
5. **Use ABAG's registered office** — Eliminates the need to arrange a separate Italian address before incorporation

---

## Concurrent Activities

While the company formation is in progress, ABAG can simultaneously:

- Begin the banking documentation preparation (so bank account opening begins as soon as the company is registered)
- Set up accounting software and systems
- Prepare employment contracts if hiring is planned immediately
- Draft and negotiate commercial leases or other agreements required before trading

This parallel approach significantly reduces total time to operational.
