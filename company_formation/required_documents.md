# Required Documents for Italian Company Formation

## Overview

This document provides a comprehensive list of documentation required for Italian company formation. The exact requirements vary by entity type and by whether shareholders are individuals or legal entities (corporations).

ABAG coordinates all document collection and provides detailed instructions for obtaining U.S. documents in the format required by Italian authorities.

---

## Part 1 — Documents from Individual Shareholders/Directors

Required for every individual who is:
- A shareholder of the Italian entity
- A director (amministratore) of the Italian entity
- A beneficial owner (titolare effettivo) if different from the above

### 1.1 Identity Documents

- [ ] **Valid passport** (color copy, signed page) — must be valid at the time of incorporation and ideally for at least 12 months beyond the incorporation date
- [ ] **National ID card** (if applicable, as a supplement)
- [ ] **Codice fiscale** (Italian tax code) — if already obtained; ABAG will apply for this if not yet held

### 1.2 Proof of Address

- [ ] **Utility bill or bank statement** (no older than 3 months) confirming current home address
- [ ] Address must match the passport or ID card; if the address has recently changed, additional documentation may be required

### 1.3 Personal Details

- [ ] Full legal name (as in passport)
- [ ] Date and place of birth
- [ ] Country of citizenship and nationality
- [ ] Current residential address
- [ ] Italian tax code (if already obtained)

---

## Part 2 — Documents from Corporate Shareholders

Required when the shareholder is a U.S. company (LLC, Corporation, etc.) or other legal entity.

### 2.1 Corporate Documents (with Apostille + Certified Italian Translation)

- [ ] **Certificate of Incorporation** (or equivalent formation document confirming the entity's existence)
- [ ] **Articles of Incorporation and/or Bylaws** (operating agreement for LLCs; articles and bylaws for corporations)
- [ ] **Certificate of Good Standing** (issued by the Secretary of State of the state of incorporation, dated within 6 months)
- [ ] **Incumbency Certificate** or equivalent, confirming current officers and directors authorized to act on behalf of the company

### 2.2 Apostille Requirements

All U.S. corporate documents submitted to Italian authorities must:
1. Be **apostilled** — obtain the apostille from the Secretary of State of the state where the document was issued
2. Be **officially translated into Italian** — by a sworn translator or a translation certified for Italian legal purposes

ABAG provides detailed guidance on obtaining apostilles and recommends trusted translators for the Italian certification process.

### 2.3 Corporate Authorization

- [ ] **Board Resolution** or written consent authorizing the establishment of the Italian entity and delegating authority to sign the incorporation deed (or granting power of attorney to ABAG)
- [ ] **Power of Attorney** (Procura Speciale) — if ABAG or an Italian professional will sign the notarial deed on behalf of the U.S. company; must be notarized and apostilled

---

## Part 3 — Documents from the U.S. Parent (if different from corporate shareholder)

If there is a U.S. holding company or controlling entity above the direct corporate shareholder:
- The same documents as Part 2 may be required
- The Italian notary and bank will conduct due diligence through the full ownership chain

---

## Part 4 — Additional Documents for Bank Account Opening

In addition to the above, Italian banks typically also require:
- [ ] Company description letter (explaining the business activity, customers, and purpose of the Italian entity)
- [ ] Source of funds declaration
- [ ] Business plan (for some banks, particularly for new companies)
- [ ] References from the U.S. company's existing bank (for some Italian banks)

See `/banking/required_documents.md` for the full banking documentation checklist.

---

## Document Format Requirements

| Requirement | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Language** | All documents submitted to Italian authorities must be in Italian or with certified Italian translation |
| **Apostille** | U.S. public documents (notarized docs, Secretary of State certificates) must be apostilled |
| **Original vs. copy** | Some documents require originals; others (notably passports) require certified copies |
| **Date validity** | Certificates of Good Standing should be issued within 6 months of submission |
| **Notarization** | Powers of attorney and some declarations must be notarized (by U.S. notary) and apostilled |
