# Italian Labor Law — Overview for U.S. Employers

## Introduction

Italian labor law is among the most protective of employees in the EU. U.S. companies hiring staff in Italy for the first time often find the system significantly more complex and employee-protective than U.S. employment law. Understanding the fundamentals is essential before making hiring decisions.

---

## Employment Contract Types

| Contract Type | Key Features |
|---|---|
| **Contratto a tempo indeterminato** | Open-ended (permanent) contract — most common and most protected |
| **Contratto a tempo determinato** | Fixed-term contract — maximum 24 months; limited to specific reasons |
| **Contratto a progetto** | Project-based; largely abolished by the Jobs Act |
| **Apprendistato** | Apprenticeship — for employees under 29; lower social security contributions |
| **Contratto part-time** | Part-time permanent or fixed-term |
| **Somministrazione** | Temporary staffing through an authorized agency |

**Key point:** The default in Italy is the open-ended permanent contract. Fixed-term contracts are permitted but restricted in duration and frequency of renewal.

---

## National Collective Bargaining Agreements (CCNL)

Italy's labor relations are governed by **National Collective Bargaining Agreements (Contratti Collettivi Nazionali di Lavoro — CCNL)**, negotiated between employer associations and trade unions for each industry sector.

CCNLs establish:
- Minimum salary levels by job classification
- Working hours and overtime rules
- Paid leave entitlements (above statutory minimums)
- Notice periods for termination
- Severance pay (TFR — Trattamento di Fine Rapporto) calculations
- Benefits such as meal vouchers, health insurance, and training

**Examples of widely applicable CCNLs:**
- CCNL Commercio (commerce and trade)
- CCNL Terziario (services)
- CCNL Metalmeccanico (manufacturing/metalworking)
- CCNL Studi Professionali (professional services)
- CCNL Information Technology

**Every Italian employer must apply the correct CCNL** for the company's sector and the employee's role. Failure to apply the correct CCNL is a significant compliance risk.

---

## Working Hours

- **Standard workweek:** 40 hours (8 hours/day, 5 days/week)
- **Maximum working time:** 48 hours per week (averaged over a reference period), including overtime
- **Overtime:** Must be agreed (in the CCNL or employment contract); overtime premium applies (typically 25%–35% extra pay)
- **Rest:** Minimum 11 hours consecutive rest between working days; minimum 24-hour rest per week

---

## Paid Leave Entitlements

| Leave Type | Minimum Entitlement |
|---|---|
| **Ferie (Annual Leave)** | 4 weeks per year (most CCNLs provide 4–5 weeks) |
| **Permessi ROL** | Reduced working hours leave — varies by CCNL |
| **Malattia (Sick Leave)** | INPS-covered; duration varies by CCNL |
| **Maternità (Maternity Leave)** | 5 months compulsory (2 months before; 3 months after birth); paid at 80% by INPS |
| **Paternità (Paternity Leave)** | 10 days compulsory (2024); partially paid by INPS |
| **Congedo parentale (Parental Leave)** | Up to 10 months between both parents; partial INPS payment |

## Salary and Social Security Contributions

### Employer Contributions

Italian employers must pay significant social security contributions (oneri sociali):

| Contribution | Approximate Rate |
|---|---|
| INPS (employee social security) | ~27–30% of gross salary |
| INAIL (workplace injury insurance) | 0.5–5% (varies by sector) |
| **Total employer cost** | ~33–40% above gross salary |

**Example:** If an employee earns €40,000 gross, the total employer cost is approximately €53,000–€56,000 including employer contributions.

### Employee Contributions

Employees also pay INPS contributions (~9.5% of gross salary), which are withheld by the employer and remitted.

### Trattamento di Fine Rapporto (TFR)

TFR is the Italian mandatory severance fund:
- Accrues at approximately **1/13.5 of annual gross salary per year**
- Paid out on termination, retirement, or resignation
- Can be transferred to INPS (Fondo INPS) or kept as a company liability
- Must be disclosed on the balance sheet as a long-term liability

## Hiring Process — Mandatory Steps

1. **Register with INPS and INAIL** (if not already done)
2. **Notify the Centro per l'Impiego** (regional employment center) — mandatory pre-employment notification
3. **Issue a written employment contract** before the employee starts
4. **Conduct safety training** (D.Lgs. 81/2008) — mandatory for all employees
5. **Medical fitness examination** (visita medica preventiva) — required for certain roles

---

## Termination of Employment

Italian employment law strongly protects employees, particularly those on open-ended contracts.

### Justified Reasons for Termination

- **Giustificato motivo oggettivo (GMO):** Objective business reason (redundancy, reorganization); severance and lengthy procedures required
- **Giustificato motivo soggettivo (GMS):** Employee-related reason falling short of misconduct
- **Giusta causa:** Serious misconduct justifying immediate dismissal without notice

### Dismissal Procedure

1. Written dismissal notice
2. Notice period (as per CCNL — typically 15 days to 3+ months depending on seniority)
3. TFR payment
4. Anti-discrimination compliance

Wrongful dismissal (licenziamento illegittimo) can result in reinstatement orders or indemnity payments of 3–27 months' salary (depending on company size and circumstances).

---

## Employment Law Compliance Tips for U.S. Employers

1. **Always apply the correct CCNL** — consult ABAG or an Italian labor lawyer to identify the right agreement
2. **Never hire without a written employment contract** issued before the start date
3. **Never delay Centro per l'Impiego notifications** — even one day late can result in fines
4. **Budget for employer social security costs** — the full cost of an employee is ~35–40% more than gross salary
5. **Termination is expensive** — factor TFR liability and potential legal costs into your employment plan
