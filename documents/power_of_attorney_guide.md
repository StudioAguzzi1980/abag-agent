# Power of Attorney — Guide for U.S. Clients

## Overview

A Power of Attorney (Procura Speciale) is a legal document that authorizes ABAG (or another designated person) to act on the client's behalf for specific purposes in Italy. For most Italian company formation and banking engagements, a Power of Attorney is required so that the client does not need to travel to Italy.

This guide explains how to prepare, notarize, and apostille a Power of Attorney in the United States for use in Italy.

---

## When Is a Power of Attorney Required?

- **Company formation:** To sign the notarial deed before the Italian notary on the client's behalf
- **Bank account opening:** To authorize an ABAG representative to sign account opening documents
- **Government filings:** For certain applications to Italian authorities
- **Real estate transactions:** For property purchases (not typically part of standard ABAG services)

ABAG will provide a draft Power of Attorney and detailed instructions when required.

---

## Types of Power of Attorney

| Type | Description |
|---|---|
| **Procura Speciale** | Limited to specific acts (e.g., incorporating a specific company) — most common for ABAG engagements |
| **Procura Generale** | General authority to act on behalf of the principal — broader and less commonly used |

ABAG standard engagements use a **Procura Speciale** limited to the specific transaction.

## Step-by-Step Guide: Preparing a U.S. Power of Attorney for Italian Use

### Step 1 — Receive the Draft from ABAG

ABAG provides a draft Power of Attorney in Italian (or bilingual Italian/English). Review it with a qualified professional if desired.

### Step 2 — Sign Before a U.S. Notary Public

Take the draft Power of Attorney to a U.S. notary public and sign it in the notary's presence. The notary will:
- Verify your identity
- Witness your signature
- Affix their notarial seal and signature

**Finding a U.S. Notary Public:**
- Banks and law firms typically have notaries
- UPS Stores and shipping centers often offer notary services
- Online notary services (Remote Online Notarization — RON) are accepted in many U.S. states

### Step 3 — Obtain State Authentication (if required)

In some states, the notary's signature must be authenticated by the County Clerk before the apostille can be issued. Check with your state's Secretary of State office whether this step is required.

### Step 4 — Obtain the Apostille

The **Apostille** is an official certification that authenticates the notary's signature for use in countries that are signatories to the **Hague Apostille Convention** (Italy is a signatory).

**Where to obtain the apostille:**
- Contact the **Secretary of State office** in the state where the document was notarized
- Each state has its own process and fees (typically $10–$50 per document)
- Some states offer expedited processing; commercial apostille services are also available

**Timeline:** 1–5 business days (regular processing); 1–2 business days (expedited)

**States with faster processing:**
- California, Texas, Florida: Check current times; online services often faster
- New York: Can be slower; commercial services recommended if urgent

### Step 5 — Send the Original to ABAG

Send the original signed and apostilled Power of Attorney to ABAG in Italy. ABAG will confirm receipt and verify the document meets Italian notarial requirements.

**Send via:** FedEx International Priority or DHL Express (for tracking and speed)
**Address:** Provided by your ABAG project manager

## Common Issues and How to Avoid Them

| Issue | Prevention |
|---|---|
| Notary seal is illegible | Ensure the notary uses a clear, embossed or inked seal |
| Wrong state issues the apostille | Apostille must be from the same state where the document was notarized |
| Document signed incorrectly | Sign exactly as your name appears in the PoA and in your passport |
| Missing authentication step | Confirm with Secretary of State whether county clerk authentication is required in your state |
| Apostille expired or incorrect date | Use the most recently issued apostille; ensure dates are consistent |
