# FAQ — Banking in Italy for Foreign Companies

**Q: Can a foreign-owned Italian company open a bank account in Italy?**
A: Yes. Foreign-owned Italian companies have the same right to open corporate bank accounts as domestically owned companies. However, in practice, Italian banks apply particularly thorough KYC and AML procedures for foreign-owned entities, which means the process can be more demanding and time-consuming. With the right preparation and relationships, it is absolutely achievable — ABAG's banking support service is specifically designed to navigate this process efficiently.

---

**Q: Why is it so difficult to open a bank account in Italy as a foreigner?**
A: Italian banks are subject to strict EU and Italian AML regulations. When the beneficial owner of a company is a non-EU national or the parent entity is a foreign corporation, banks must conduct enhanced due diligence. This involves:
- Verifying the identity and background of all beneficial owners
- Understanding the source of funds and purpose of the account
- Assessing compliance with AML regulations
- Internal approval processes that can be slow and opaque

Without established relationships and local expertise, this process can take months or fail entirely.

---

**Q: Does my company need to be fully incorporated before opening a bank account?**
A: Yes. Italian banks typically require:
- A completed registration in the Italian Business Register (Visura Camerale)
- A valid Italian tax code (codice fiscale) for the company
- A VAT number (Partita IVA) from the Agenzia delle Entrate
- A registered Italian address

ABAG coordinates the timing of bank account opening to follow immediately after incorporation is complete.

**Q: What documents are typically required to open an Italian corporate bank account?**
A: Requirements vary by bank, but the standard documentation package includes:
- Company incorporation documents (notarial deed, bylaws)
- Business Register extract (Visura Camerale)
- Valid passport for all directors, authorized signatories, and beneficial owners
- Proof of address for all key individuals
- Description of the company's business activities
- Source of funds declaration
- For U.S. parent companies: U.S. corporate documents with apostille and Italian translation

See `/banking/required_documents.md` for a complete checklist.

**Q: Can I open an Italian bank account remotely (without traveling to Italy)?**
A: Some banks offer remote or partially remote account opening procedures for foreign clients, particularly for digital or international banks. Traditional Italian banks typically prefer or require at least one in-person meeting with a bank representative, though this is often manageable via a power of attorney or by visiting the bank during a trip to Italy. **Q: Can I use a fintech (Wise, Revolut, N26) instead of a traditional Italian bank?**
A: Fintech solutions can be a useful **interim or supplementary** solution while a traditional bank account is being established. Platforms like Wise Business and Revolut Business are regulated as Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) and can hold EUR balances, receive SEPA payments, and process international transfers. However, they may not be accepted by all Italian suppliers or counterparties, and some do not support Italian-specific requirements such as electronic invoicing (fatturazione elettronica). ABAG recommends establishing a traditional Italian corporate bank account as the primary account, with fintech as a complement.

---

**Q: What is IBAN and do Italian companies have one?**
A: Yes. Italian corporate bank accounts are identified by an **IBAN (International Bank Account Number)**, which begins with "IT" followed by check digits and the account details. Italian IBANs are 27 characters long and are required for SEPA credit transfers and direct debits, which are standard in the EU payment system.
