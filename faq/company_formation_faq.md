# FAQ — Company Formation in Italy

**Q: What is the most common type of Italian company for U.S. businesses?**
A: The **S.r.l. (Società a Responsabilità Limitata)** is by far the most common structure for foreign-owned businesses in Italy. It is equivalent to a U.S. LLC — a limited liability company that is flexible, relatively straightforward to set up, and widely recognized by Italian banks, tax authorities, and business counterparties.

---

**Q: What is the minimum share capital for an Italian S.r.l.?**
A: For a standard S.r.l., the minimum share capital is €10,000 (of which at least 25% must be paid in at incorporation, i.e., €2,500). For a simplified S.r.l. (S.r.l.s.), the minimum share capital can be as low as €1, though this structure has more restrictions on share transfer and governance. For most U.S. businesses, a standard S.r.l. is recommended.

---

**Q: Can a U.S. company be the sole shareholder of an Italian S.r.l.?**
A: Yes. A U.S. corporation, LLC, or any other legal entity can be the 100% shareholder of an Italian S.r.l. (or S.p.A.). There is no requirement for an Italian or EU shareholder.

---

**Q: Can a U.S. citizen be a director of an Italian company?**
A: Yes. There is no citizenship or residency requirement for directors of Italian companies. However, all directors must obtain an Italian tax code (codice fiscale) — which ABAG arranges as part of the incorporation process.

---

**Q: Do I need to travel to Italy to incorporate a company?**
A: In most cases, no. ABAG can manage the entire incorporation process remotely by acting under a power of attorney granted by the client. The power of attorney must be notarized and apostilled in the U.S. — ABAG provides detailed instructions for this. In some circumstances, a notarized video appearance may be required.

---

**Q: How long does it take to incorporate an Italian company?**
A: A typical S.r.l. incorporation takes **3 to 6 weeks** from the time all required documents are received and in order. The main variables are the time required to obtain Italian tax codes, the availability of the Italian notary, and the current processing times at the Chamber of Commerce and Agenzia delle Entrate.

---

**Q: What documents do I need to provide?**
A: For U.S. clients, the typical documentation required includes:
- Valid passport copies for all directors and shareholders (individuals)
- Proof of address for all directors and shareholders
- For corporate shareholders: Certificate of Incorporation, Articles, Certificate of Good Standing — with apostille and certified Italian translation
- Power of attorney authorizing ABAG to act on your behalf (notarized and apostilled)

See `/company_formation/required_documents.md` for a complete checklist.

---

**Q: What is the difference between a subsidiary and a branch office?**
A: A **subsidiary** (e.g., an Italian S.r.l.) is a separate legal entity from the U.S. parent — it has its own legal personality, limited liability, and separate tax identity. A **branch office** is an extension of the U.S. parent operating in Italy, with no separate legal identity — the parent company bears full liability for the branch's obligations. For most clients, a subsidiary (S.r.l.) is the preferred structure.

---

**Q: What is a registered office and do I need a physical office in Italy?**
A: Every Italian company must have a registered address (sede legale) in Italy. This is the official address registered with the Chamber of Commerce and used for all correspondence with Italian authorities. A physical operational office is not required for registration. ABAG can provide a registered office address as part of its services.

---

**Q: What happens after incorporation is complete?**
A: After the company is registered, the typical next steps are:
1. Open a corporate bank account
2. Register for INPS (social security) if hiring employees
3. Register for any required sector-specific licenses
4. Set up accounting systems
5. Issue the first invoices and begin trading

ABAG assists with all of these post-incorporation steps.
