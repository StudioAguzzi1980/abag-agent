# FAQ — Corporate Compliance in Italy

**Q: What are the main ongoing compliance obligations for an Italian S.r.l.?**
A: Once incorporated, an Italian S.r.l. has the following key recurring obligations:

**Annual:**
- Hold a shareholders' meeting to approve the annual financial statements within 120 days of year-end (or 180 days in some cases)
- File the approved financial statements with the Chamber of Commerce within 30 days of shareholder approval
- File the annual corporate income tax return (Modello Redditi SC) — due within 11 months of year-end
- Pay IRES and IRAP advance payments in June and November

**Monthly / Quarterly:**
- File and pay VAT returns
- Submit payroll tax declarations (F24) and pay social security contributions (INPS) if the company has employees
- Issue and process electronic invoices (fatturazione elettronica)

**Ongoing:**
- Maintain accurate accounting records
- Update the Business Register within 30 days of any corporate changes (directors, shareholders, address, capital)
- Comply with GDPR data protection obligations
- Comply with AML requirements (beneficial ownership registration)

---

**Q: What is the Registro delle Imprese (Business Register)?**
A: The Registro delle Imprese is Italy's official Business Register, maintained by local Chambers of Commerce. Every Italian company is registered here, and the register contains key corporate information including incorporation documents, directors, shareholders, registered address, and financial statements. Changes to this information must be filed promptly — typically within 30 days of the change.

---

**Q: What is the Italian equivalent of a corporate minute book?**
A: Italian companies are required to maintain a Libro dei Verbali (book of minutes) recording the resolutions of shareholders' meetings and (where applicable) board of directors' meetings. The minute book must be kept at the company's registered office and maintained in Italian. For S.r.l. companies, shareholder and director decisions can also be documented as written resolutions (decisioni dei soci per iscritto) without a formal meeting in many cases.

---

**Q: Does an Italian S.r.l. need statutory auditors?**
A: An Italian S.r.l. is required to appoint a statutory auditor (sindaco) or a Board of Statutory Auditors (Collegio Sindacale) if any one of the following thresholds is met for two consecutive years:
- Total assets > €4.4 million
- Revenue > €8.8 million
- Employees > 50

Smaller S.r.l. companies are generally not required to appoint statutory auditors, though they may voluntarily do so. If an S.r.l. is required to appoint auditors, it must also have its accounts audited by a statutory auditor.

**Q: What is GDPR and does it apply to my Italian company?**
A: Yes. The **General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR — EU Regulation 2016/679)** applies to any organization that processes the personal data of individuals in the EU, regardless of where the organization is based. Your Italian company will process personal data (of employees, clients, suppliers, website users), and is therefore subject to GDPR. Key requirements include:
- Maintaining records of processing activities
- Having a valid legal basis for processing
- Responding to data subject rights requests
- Reporting data breaches within 72 hours
- Appointing a Data Protection Officer (DPO) if required

Italy's supervisory authority is the **Garante per la Protezione dei Dati Personali** (Garante Privacy).

---

**Q: What is D.Lgs. 231/2001 and does it affect my Italian company?**
A: D.Lgs. 231/2001 is the Italian law that introduced corporate criminal liability — meaning that a company (not just its individual employees) can be held liable for certain criminal offenses committed by its directors, employees, or agents. Offenses covered include corruption, fraud, market manipulation, and environmental crimes, among others.

Companies can protect themselves by adopting and effectively implementing an **Organizational, Management, and Control Model (Modello 231)** and appointing a **Supervisory Body (Organismo di Vigilanza)**. While not legally mandatory for all companies, the Modello 231 is strongly recommended for foreign-owned Italian entities, particularly those in regulated sectors or above a certain size.

**Q: What happens if my Italian company misses a compliance deadline?**
A: Penalties for missing Italian compliance deadlines vary by obligation:
- **Late VAT payment:** Penalty of 30% of unpaid tax (reducible via voluntary disclosure)
- **Late annual account filing:** Administrative fine from €103 to €1,032 per director
- **Late tax return:** Penalty of 30% of tax due plus interest
- **Late Business Register filing:** Administrative fine
