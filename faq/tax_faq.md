# FAQ — Italian Tax for U.S. Companies

**Q: What taxes does an Italian S.r.l. (subsidiary) have to pay?**
A: An Italian S.r.l. is subject to the following main taxes:
- **IRES (Corporate Income Tax):** 24% on taxable profits
- **IRAP (Regional Production Tax):** Standard rate of 3.9% on adjusted production value (varies slightly by region)
- **IVA (VAT):** Standard rate of 22%; reduced rates of 10%, 5%, and 4% apply to specific goods and services

---

**Q: Does Italy and the U.S. have a tax treaty to avoid double taxation?**
A: Yes. The **Convention for the Avoidance of Double Taxation** between the U.S. and Italy was signed in 1984 and updated by protocol in 1999. It provides for:
- Reduced withholding taxes on dividends (5% for qualifying corporate shareholders; 15% otherwise)
- Reduced withholding on interest (10%)
- Reduced withholding on royalties (8%)
- Business profit taxed only in Italy if the U.S. company has a permanent establishment (PE) in Italy
- Relief mechanisms to prevent the same income from being taxed twice in both countries

---

**Q: What is a "permanent establishment" (PE) and why does it matter?**
A: A permanent establishment (PE) is a fixed place of business through which a company carries out all or part of its business activity in another country. If a U.S. company is found to have a PE in Italy (e.g., a fixed office, a dependent agent authorized to conclude contracts), Italy has the right to tax the profits attributable to that PE. Establishing an S.r.l. subsidiary generally resolves PE risk for the subsidiary's own activities, but the U.S. parent must still be careful not to create a separate PE through its own activities in Italy.

---

**Q: What is VAT (IVA) and when does my Italian company need to register for it?**
A: Italian VAT (IVA — Imposta sul Valore Aggiunto) is a consumption tax charged on most sales of goods and services. Italian companies must register for VAT with the Agenzia delle Entrate at the time of incorporation. VAT returns must be filed monthly or quarterly, and net VAT due (output tax minus input tax recovered) must be paid. Electronic invoicing (fatturazione elettronica) via the government's Sistema di Interscambio (SDI) platform is mandatory for B2B transactions in Italy.

---

**Q: What are the transfer pricing rules for intracompany transactions?**
A: Italy follows the OECD arm's length principle for transfer pricing. Any transactions between the Italian subsidiary and its U.S. parent (or related entities) — such as management fees, royalties, intercompany loans, or goods sales — must be priced as if they were between unrelated parties. Italian law requires formal transfer pricing documentation for companies above certain thresholds, and penalties for non-compliance can be significant. ABAG recommends preparing transfer pricing documentation before the first intragroup transaction occurs.

---

**Q: What is the Regime dei Neo-Residenti (New Residents Flat Tax)?**
A: Italy offers a **€100,000 per year flat tax** on all foreign-source income for individuals who establish their tax residency in Italy and have not been Italian tax residents for the 9 of the 10 years preceding their move. This regime, introduced in 2017, can be highly advantageous for high-net-worth U.S. individuals relocating to Italy. It applies for a maximum of 15 years. Family members can be included for an additional €25,000 per person per year.

---

**Q: Does my Italian company need to file U.S. tax forms?**
A: If a U.S. company or individual owns the Italian entity, there may be U.S. filing requirements:
- **Form 5471** — Required for U.S. shareholders of foreign corporations
- **FBAR (FinCEN 114)** — Required if bank accounts exceed $10,000
- **FATCA (Form 8938)** — Required for specified foreign financial assets above thresholds
- **Subpart F / GILTI** — Potential current U.S. taxation on certain passive or high-return foreign income
- **Form 8858** — For U.S. owners of foreign disregarded entities

**Q: When are Italian corporate tax returns due?**
A: Italian corporate tax returns (Modello Redditi SC) are generally due within **11 months of the end of the fiscal year**. For companies with a December 31 year-end, this means the return is due by **November 30** of the following year. Advance tax payments are made in June (first installment) and November (second installment).

---

**Q: Is there a reduced corporate tax rate available in Italy?**
A: Yes, several incentive regimes can effectively reduce the tax burden:
- **Patent Box** — 50% partial exemption on income from qualifying intellectual property
- **ACE (Aiuto alla Crescita Economica)** — Deduction for equity increases (subject to current-year rules)
- **R&D Tax Credit** — Credit of 20% on qualifying R&D expenditure
- **ZES/SEZ** — Reduced IRES rates in Special Economic Zones in Southern Italy
- **Startup regime** — Innovative startups benefit from reduced administrative costs and investor deductions
