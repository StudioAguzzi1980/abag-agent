# Italian Tax System — Overview

## Introduction

Italy's tax system is administered primarily by the **Agenzia delle Entrate** (Italian Revenue Agency) and enforced by the **Guardia di Finanza** (financial police). For foreign-owned businesses and U.S. companies in particular, understanding the Italian tax landscape is essential before establishing a presence.

Italy's system combines corporate taxes, regional taxes, value-added tax (VAT), and withholding taxes — each with their own bases, rates, and compliance requirements. Cross-border transactions involving U.S. parent companies add a further layer of complexity that requires careful planning.

---

## Key Taxes at a Glance

| Tax | Full Name | Rate | Who Pays |
|---|---|---|---|
| IRES | Imposta sul Reddito delle Società | 24% | Italian resident companies; PE of foreign companies |
| IRAP | Imposta Regionale sulle Attività Produttive | 3.9% (standard) | Italian companies and individuals with business income |
| IVA | Imposta sul Valore Aggiunto (VAT) | 22% / 10% / 5% / 4% | Companies making taxable supplies |
| IRPEF | Imposta sul Reddito delle Persone Fisiche | 23%–43% (progressive) | Individuals on personal income |
| Imposta di Registro | Registration Tax | Various (0.5%–15%) | On registered deeds and contracts |
| IMU | Imposta Municipale Propria | 0.4%–1.06% | Owners of Italian real property |

## The Italian Fiscal Year

Italy's fiscal year typically runs from **January 1 to December 31**, though companies may elect a different fiscal year. Most foreign subsidiaries align with the parent company's year.

## Electronic Filing and Invoicing

Italy has been a pioneer in mandatory electronic tax procedures:

- **Fatturazione Elettronica** — All B2B and B2G invoices in Italy must be issued electronically via the government's Sistema di Interscambio (SDI) platform
- **Electronic VAT returns** — All VAT returns and communications are submitted electronically
- **Electronic accounting communications** — Pre-filled VAT returns (bozze IVA) are generated from SDI data
- **Tax drawer (Cassetto Fiscale)** — Each taxpayer has an online account with the Agenzia delle Entrate where all tax positions and communications are visible

## U.S.–Italy Cross-Border Tax Considerations

For U.S. companies with Italian operations, the key cross-border tax issues include:

1. **Permanent Establishment (PE) risk** — Physical presence or dependent agents in Italy may create Italian tax nexus for the U.S. parent
2. **Transfer pricing** — All intercompany transactions must be arm's length and documented
3. **Withholding taxes** — Dividends, interest, and royalties paid from Italy to the U.S. parent are subject to withholding (reduced by treaty)
4. **CFC/GILTI** — U.S. shareholders of foreign corporations face potential current U.S. taxation under Subpart F and GILTI
5. **Double taxation relief** — The U.S.–Italy Tax Treaty and U.S. foreign tax credit mechanisms prevent (most) double taxation
