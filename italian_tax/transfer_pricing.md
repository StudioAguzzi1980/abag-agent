# Transfer Pricing in Italy

## Overview

Transfer pricing refers to the pricing of transactions between **related parties** (e.g., a U.S. parent company and its Italian subsidiary). Italian and international tax rules require that these transactions be priced as if they were between **unrelated, independent parties** — the so-called **arm's length principle**.

Italy's transfer pricing rules are set out in Article 110(7) of the Italian Tax Code (TUIR) and are aligned with the **OECD Transfer Pricing Guidelines for Multinational Enterprises and Tax Administrations**.

---

## Why Transfer Pricing Matters

For U.S. companies with Italian subsidiaries, virtually every intercompany transaction is subject to transfer pricing rules:
- Management fees charged by the U.S. parent to the Italian subsidiary
- Royalties for use of the U.S. parent's intellectual property (trademarks, patents, software)
- Intercompany loans and interest payments
- Sales of goods between the U.S. parent and Italian entity
- Provision of services between group companies

If the Italian tax authority (Agenzia delle Entrate) believes that intercompany pricing does not reflect arm's length conditions, it may adjust the Italian entity's taxable income — potentially creating double taxation and significant penalties.

---

## The Arm's Length Principle

The arm's length price is the price that **independent parties would agree to** under comparable circumstances. In practice, this is determined by:

1. Identifying comparable transactions between unrelated parties
2. Applying an appropriate transfer pricing method
3. Establishing a price range (arm's length range) within which the intercompany price should fall

---

## OECD Transfer Pricing Methods

| Method | Best Used For |
|---|---|
| **Comparable Uncontrolled Price (CUP)** | Commodity transactions, intercompany loans, royalties with comparable third-party data |
| **Cost Plus Method (CPM)** | Manufacturing or routine service providers |
| **Resale Price Method (RPM)** | Distribution entities |
| **Transactional Net Margin Method (TNMM)** | Most common — used for routine functions (distributors, service providers, manufacturers) |
| **Profit Split Method (PSM)** | Complex arrangements where both parties contribute significant intangible assets |

The TNMM is the most widely used method in Italian transfer pricing audits and documentation.

## Italian Documentation Requirements

Italy follows the **OECD 3-tier documentation standard**:

### 1. Master File (Masterfile)
High-level description of the multinational group:
- Group structure and ownership chart
- Description of the group's businesses
- Group-level intangible asset policy
- Intercompany financing arrangements
- Annual consolidated financial statements

### 2. Local File (Documentazione Nazionale)
Italian entity-specific documentation:
- Description of the Italian entity and its functions, assets, and risks
- List of all intercompany transactions with amounts
- Economic analysis for each material transaction type (benchmarking)
- Selected transfer pricing method and justification
- Financial data used in the analysis

### 3. Country-by-Country Report (CbCR)
Required for groups with consolidated revenue above **€750 million**:
- Revenue, profit, taxes paid, and employees in each country
- Submitted to the Agenzia delle Entrate by the Italian entity (or notified if submitted by the parent)

---

## When Documentation Is Required

Italian transfer pricing documentation is **not legally mandatory** for all companies. However:
- Companies that have documentation in place benefit from **penalty protection** — if the Italian tax authority makes a transfer pricing adjustment, the penalty is waived if the company can show it had appropriate documentation at the time of filing
- Without documentation, penalties of **90%–180%** of additional tax due can apply
- Given the penalty exposure, ABAG strongly recommends documentation for any company with material intercompany transactions

---

## Italian Tax Authority Focus Areas

The Agenzia delle Entrate's transfer pricing audits frequently focus on:
- Royalties paid to U.S. IP holding companies
- Management fees charged to Italian subsidiaries
- Intercompany loans with below-market interest rates
- Italian distributors with artificially compressed margins
- Corporate restructurings that shift value out of Italy

---

## Practical Steps for U.S. Companies

1. **Identify all intercompany transactions** — before the Italian company is operational
2. **Set intercompany prices** — based on arm's length analysis before transactions occur
3. **Prepare documentation** — before filing the first Italian tax return
4. **Review annually** — update documentation when business circumstances change materially
5. **Consider an Advance Pricing Agreement (APA)** — for material ongoing transactions, an APA with the Agenzia delle Entrate provides certainty for 3–5 years
