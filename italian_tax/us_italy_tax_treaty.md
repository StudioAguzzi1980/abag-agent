# U.S.–Italy Double Taxation Treaty

## Overview

The **Convention Between the United States of America and the Republic of Italy for the Avoidance of Double Taxation and the Prevention of Fiscal Evasion with Respect to Taxes on Income** was originally signed on **April 17, 1984**, and amended by a Protocol signed on **August 25, 1999** (effective 2009).

The treaty is the primary legal framework governing the taxation of income flowing between the U.S. and Italy, and is essential for any U.S.-owned business operating in Italy.

---

## Key Purpose

The treaty's primary objectives are to:

1. **Prevent double taxation** — Ensure that the same income is not taxed twice (once in Italy and once in the U.S.)
2. **Prevent tax evasion** — Prevent misuse of the treaty to avoid all taxation
3. **Provide certainty** — Give businesses and individuals a clear framework for understanding their tax obligations in cross-border situations

---

## Withholding Tax Rates Under the Treaty

| Income Type | Default Rate | Treaty Rate (Business) | Treaty Rate (Portfolio) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Dividends** | 26% (IT domestic) | **5%** (≥25% ownership) | **15%** (other cases) |
| **Interest** | 26% (IT domestic) | **10%** | **10%** |
| **Royalties** | 30% (IT domestic) | **8%** | **8%** |

### Dividend Withholding
- **5%** applies when the beneficial owner is a corporation that holds at least **25%** of the voting stock of the paying Italian company
- **15%** applies in all other cases (individuals, portfolio investors, below 25% threshold)
- Certain Italian distributions may qualify for the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive (reducing withholding to **0%**) if the EU group structure qualifies

### Interest Withholding
- 10% applies on interest payments to U.S. residents
- Some types of interest are exempt (e.g., on government securities, if specifically negotiated)

### Royalties Withholding
- 8% applies on royalties for the use of intellectual property (patents, trademarks, software)
- A key planning point: Italy's domestic rate of 30% makes the treaty reduction significant

## Permanent Establishment (PE) Rules

Under the treaty, Italy may tax the business profits of a U.S. company **only if** that company has a **permanent establishment (PE)** in Italy.

### What Constitutes a PE?
- A **fixed place of business** — office, factory, warehouse, workshop, branch
- A **construction site** lasting more than 12 months
- A **dependent agent** — a person in Italy who habitually concludes contracts on behalf of the U.S. company
- A **service PE** — provision of services in Italy for more than 183 days in any 12-month period

### What Does NOT Create a PE?
- Use of facilities solely for storage, display, or delivery of goods
- Maintenance of a fixed place solely for purchasing goods or collecting information
- Independent agents acting in their ordinary course of business
- Preparatory or auxiliary activities

### Why This Matters
If a U.S. company has a PE in Italy, Italy can tax the profits attributable to that PE. This can result in double taxation if the same profits are also taxed in the U.S. (though the treaty provides for foreign tax credits to mitigate this).

Most U.S. companies avoid this issue by establishing an Italian subsidiary (S.r.l.), which is a separate taxpayer — but care must be taken that the parent's activities in Italy don't independently create a PE.

## Residence and Competent Authority

### Individual Residence
The treaty contains a tie-breaker for individuals who might be resident in both countries (e.g., Italian-Americans), based on permanent home, center of vital interests, habitual abode, and nationality.

### Corporate Residence
A company is resident where it is incorporated or where its place of effective management (sede di direzione effettiva) is located. This matters for access to treaty benefits.

---

## Relief Mechanisms — Foreign Tax Credit

The treaty provides that both countries will give credit for taxes paid in the other country to avoid double taxation:

- **Italy:** Credits foreign taxes against Italian IRES/IRPEF
- **U.S.:** Credits Italian taxes paid against U.S. federal income tax (via IRS Form 1116 or 1118)

The foreign tax credit is subject to limitations — particularly the "limitation on benefits" (LOB) provision, which prevents treaty shopping by third-country residents.

---

## Limitation on Benefits (LOB)

The treaty includes a LOB provision (Article 17) that restricts treaty benefits to qualifying persons:
- U.S. or Italian citizens or residents meeting specified tests
- Publicly traded companies
- Companies with an active business in the residence country
- Other qualifying persons

Companies set up purely to access treaty benefits (treaty shopping) are denied treaty protection.

---

## Practical Applications for U.S. Companies

| Scenario | Treaty Relevance |
|---|---|
| Italian S.r.l. pays dividends to U.S. parent | Reduced withholding rate (5% or 15%) |
| U.S. parent charges management fee to Italian subsidiary | Royalty/service fee withholding rate; PE risk if personnel present |
| Italian subsidiary pays interest on loan from U.S. parent | 10% withholding applies |
| U.S. company sells goods to Italy without PE | No Italian corporate tax on profits |
| U.S. employee temporarily working in Italy | May be subject to Italian personal tax; treaty Article 15 applies |
