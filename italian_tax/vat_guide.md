# Italian VAT Guide (IVA — Imposta sul Valore Aggiunto)

## Overview

Italian VAT (IVA — Imposta sul Valore Aggiunto) is an indirect tax on the consumption of goods and services. It is applied at each stage of the supply chain, with businesses acting as collection agents for the tax authority. Only the final consumer bears the economic cost of VAT.

Italy's VAT system is based on **EU VAT Directive 2006/112/EC** and is broadly consistent with VAT systems across the EU.

---

## VAT Rates

| Rate | Category |
|---|---|
| **22%** | Standard rate — applies to most goods and services |
| **10%** | Reduced rate — accommodation, certain food products, live events, some construction |
| **5%** | Super-reduced rate — social services, certain food supplements |
| **4%** | Minimum rate — basic foodstuffs, books, newspapers, certain medical devices, primary homes |
| **0%** | Exempt / Zero-rated — exports, intra-EU B2B supplies (subject to conditions) |

---

## VAT Registration (Partita IVA)

Every Italian company must register for VAT with the **Agenzia delle Entrate** at the time of incorporation. The VAT number (Partita IVA) is also the company's general tax identification number and is required for:
- Issuing invoices
- Electronic invoicing (fatturazione elettronica)
- Intrastat declarations
- Bank account opening

**Format:** IT + 11 digits (e.g., IT12345678901)

Non-resident companies making taxable supplies in Italy may need to register for Italian VAT, even without an Italian legal entity, depending on the nature of their activities.

---

## How VAT Works

1. **Output VAT** — Charged on sales invoices (your customers pay this)
2. **Input VAT** — Paid on purchase invoices (you pay this to your suppliers)
3. **Net VAT payable** — Output VAT minus deductible input VAT = amount due to (or refundable from) the Agenzia delle Entrate

If input VAT exceeds output VAT (e.g., for new businesses with high startup costs), the excess is a **VAT credit** that can be used to offset future VAT liabilities or (with certain procedures) requested as a cash refund.

---

## Electronic Invoicing (Fatturazione Elettronica)

Since January 2019, electronic invoicing has been **mandatory** for all B2B and B2G transactions in Italy. Key points:

- All invoices must be transmitted via the government's **Sistema di Interscambio (SDI)** platform in XML format
- The Agenzia delle Entrate pre-validates all invoices; invalid invoices are rejected
- Paper invoices to Italian B2B counterparties are not legally valid
- B2C (consumer) transactions above €400 (and optionally below) also require electronic invoicing
- Foreign companies (non-Italian VAT registration) are exempt from SDI but may opt in voluntarily

## VAT Filing Obligations

| Obligation | Frequency | Deadline |
|---|---|---|
| Periodic VAT communication (liquidazione IVA) | Monthly or quarterly | 16th of following month (monthly) / last day of following month (quarterly) |
| Annual VAT return (dichiarazione IVA) | Annual | February 28 (for prior year) |
| Intrastat declaration | Monthly or quarterly | 25th of following month |
| Annual VAT summary (Esterometro) | Abolished — replaced by SDI | — |

**Monthly vs. quarterly:** Companies with annual taxable turnover above €400,000 (goods) or €700,000 (services) must file monthly. Smaller companies may file quarterly.

---

## Intrastat Declarations

Companies making intra-EU acquisitions or supplies of goods above certain thresholds must file **Intrastat** declarations with the Agenzia delle Dogane (Customs Agency). These declarations report intra-EU trade flows and are used by EU member states to monitor VAT compliance on cross-border transactions.

---

## VAT on Cross-Border Transactions

### Exports (Outside EU)
Exports of goods to non-EU countries (including the U.S.) are **zero-rated** — no Italian VAT is charged, but input VAT on related costs is fully recoverable.

### Intra-EU Supplies
Supplies of goods to VAT-registered businesses in other EU member states are **zero-rated** under the intra-EU supply rules, provided certain conditions are met (both parties are VAT-registered, the goods physically move to another EU country, proof of transport is maintained).

### Services to Non-EU Entities
Under the **reverse charge** mechanism, services supplied to non-EU business customers (e.g., a U.S. parent company) are generally outside the scope of Italian VAT — the customer accounts for VAT in their own jurisdiction.

---

## VAT Compliance Best Practices

1. Register for VAT at incorporation — do not begin trading before registration
2. Ensure your accounting system is configured for SDI electronic invoicing
3. Maintain clear records of input and output VAT by rate
4. Reconcile your VAT position monthly before filing
5. Understand when input VAT is not recoverable (e.g., passenger vehicles, entertainment)
6. Review cross-border transaction VAT treatment carefully before invoicing
